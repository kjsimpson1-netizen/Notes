## Rules
### Hydrogen
Hydrogen will always be the terminating atom
### Stability
The most stable [[Chemistry/Molecular Structure/Lewis Structures/Resonance]] of the [[Chemistry/Atomic Structure and Properties/Bond/Covalent Compounds]] has:
- The least non-zero [[Formal Charge]]
- The negative [[Formal Charge]] matching with the atom with the highest [[Chemistry/Atomic Structure and Properties/Periodic Properties/Electronegativity]]
- The positive [[Formal Charge]] matching with the atom with the least [[Chemistry/Atomic Structure and Properties/Periodic Properties/Electronegativity]]
## Tips
There are general trends in the groups, that occur in all elements of that group unless [[Hyper Valence]] is used
### Group 13 (Boron)
>[!Note]
>Elements in this group <u>do not</u> have to follow the [[Octet Rule]], and are also happy with a sextet
### Group 14 (Carbon)
- Carbon always has 4 bonds with no lone pairs
### Group 15 (Nitrogen)
Nitrogen has:
- If the [[Formal Charge]] is 0, 3 bonds+1 pair
- If the [[Formal Charge]] is +1, 4 bonds+0 lone pairs
### Group 16 (Oxygen)
- If the [[Formal Charge]] is 0, there are 2 bonds+2 lone pairs
- If the [[Formal Charge]] is-1, there are 1 bond+3 lone pairs
- If the [[Formal Charge]] is +1, there are 3 bonds+1 lone pair
### Group 17
- These never want to have a positive charge
### Radicals
These are any molecules that have an odd number of electrons and therefore they will have an unpaired electron in their [[Lewis Structures]].
## Steps
1. Count the number of valence electrons in the molecule or ion
2. Draw the skeletal structure of the molecule
   a) The least electronegative atom is generally the central atom
   b) Hydrogen is **ALWAYS** a terminal ion
   c) Unless told otherwise, do not form rings
3. Place 2 electrons in each bond of the skeletal structure (represented by lines)
4. Place the remaining valence electrons not accounted for in step 3 as lone pairs on individual atoms until the octet rule is satisfied
5. Form multiple bonds as needed to complete octets and account for all [[Chemistry/Basic Chemistry/Valence Electrons]]
6. Label the formal charges
   - The sum of formal charges is always equal to the overall molecular charge
## Evaluating Lewis Structures
1. Do all atoms have octets/duets/sextets?
2. Are [[Formal Charge]] minimized?
   - Can't always make them zero, but we want them as low as possible
   - Minimize total number of [[Formal Charge]]
3. Are the charges on the right atoms?
