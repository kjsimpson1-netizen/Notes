This is when there are multiple different [[Lewis Structures]] for [[Chemistry/Atomic Structure and Properties/Bond/Covalent Compounds]] but they have different bond configurations with the exact same atom placement. 
The [[Lewis Structures]] are a localized picture of the electrons, but in reality they are delocalized and therefore we do not know exactly where the electrons are. The [[Lewis Structures]] fail to show the actual probabilities of the positions of the electrons.
>[!Note]
>In chem-154, only resonances with a positive or neutral charge on the central atom will be considered valid

## Inequivalent Resonances
Inequivalent resonances are when the resonances are not completely symmetrical, and there are different options for the double bonds between different molecules, and therefore one is more likely than the others because the [[Formal Charge]] will not be optimized for all the options. 
## Equivalent/Hybrid Resonances
Equivalent resonances are when the resonances are completely symmetrical, and therefore it isn't actual any of the resonances that is the most accurate picture, but rather an average of the resonances. This is done by having a dotted line represent a partial [[Chemistry/Atomic Structure and Properties/Bond/Bond]]
![[OzoneResonance.png]]
For equivalent resonances, the [[Skeletal Structure]] must stay the same as well as the number of electrons, the electrons can only move around (double bonds, single pairs etc). The charge must therefore be the same. 
## Stability
Some inequivalent resonances do not have the same stability as the rest of them, due to the [[Formal Charge]] on the individual atoms. All of the resonances still exist, but there are some that will make up a larger portion of the overall sample based upon their stability.
