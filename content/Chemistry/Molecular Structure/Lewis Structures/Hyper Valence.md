Periods 3 and below of the [[Chemistry/Basic Chemistry/Periodic Table]] do not necessarily have to follow the [[Octet Rule]], and rather they can overfill their [[Chemistry/Basic Chemistry/Valence Electrons]] shell if they are the central atom. The reason that hyper-valence can exist is because the 3d orbital is lower energy than than 4s, so if absolutely necessary, we can overflow into the 3d orbital (it will take significantly more energy than the 3p orbital). For all the elements below period 3, they will access the s orbital, then the d orbital, then the p orbital, then the next d orbital if absolutely necessary.
>[!Note 1]
>The [[Octet Rule]] is not broken unless completely necessary. Also, only central atoms can have hyper-valence, and terminal atoms cannot.

>[!Note 2]
>For neutral atoms, the 4s orbital is filled before the 3d orbital; however, during ionization, the 3d orbital is emptied before the 4s orbital. 

The maximum number of hyper valence that a specific atom can have has to do with what group of the [[Chemistry/Basic Chemistry/Periodic Table]] that it belongs to. If it is from group 13, then it has 3 [[Chemistry/Basic Chemistry/Valence Electrons]] and this means that it can have 3 bonded pairs. This means that anything from group 13 can have a maximum of 6 valence electrons (assuming it is from period 3 or below).
