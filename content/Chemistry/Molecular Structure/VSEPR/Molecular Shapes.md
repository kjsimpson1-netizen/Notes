These are the shapes that result from removing bonded pairs and replacing them with lone pairs. The have the same general shape as the [[Parent shapes]], but missing "arms". 
## Electron repulsion
When removing lone electron pairs, it is not always easy to choose whether or not it's easier to remove the axial or the equatorial electrons. 
The electrons in two different bond pairs are going to repulse weaker than a lone pair and a bond pair, which again repulses weaker than a lone pair and a lone pair. This is because lone pairs will overlap more because the electrons are less bound than electrons in bond pairs. 
## Bond Angles
Whenever a bonded pair is replaced with a lone pair, this will push the other bonds closer together. This is because the repulsion from the lone pair is greater than that of the bonded pair, as they are less bound. So we can then state that the angle is less that that of the [[Parent shapes]].
The other thing that will effect the initial angles from the [[Parent shapes]] is the [[Chemistry/Molecular Structure/Lewis Structures/Formal Charge]] on the terrestrial atoms. If there is a charge on one of these atoms, it will repel the other bonded pairs, making the angle larger.
