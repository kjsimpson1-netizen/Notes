This is when there are branches that are formed off of the main polymer. This can take multiple forms based on the polymer, such as star, comb and branched, among many more. Increased branching increases the entanglement of these bonds, and this will cause the viscosity of the polymer of the molecule to increase. 
## Glass Transition Point
This will also cause them to have a lower [[Chemistry/Polymers/Entropic Elasticity]] point, and therefore be more likely to be pliable at room temperature. 
## Solubility
This will also increase the solubility of the polymer, as there is more space in between the molecules to allow the solvent to penetrate it. 
## Stiffness
The stiffness will depend on the degree of the branching. If there is low branching overall, there is no increase in the stiffness, and rather it becomes quite flexible; however, if there is a high degree of branching, it can actually decrease the flexibility and increase the stiffness overall. 
## Density
Overall these polymers will have a lower density because they will have a less densely packed structure compared to stacked [[Chemistry/Polymers/Architecture/Linear]]. 
## Viscosity
The inter-connected structure of the branched chains will cause entanglement, and therefore increase the velocity.
