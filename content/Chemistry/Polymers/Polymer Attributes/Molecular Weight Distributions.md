Synthetic [[Chemistry/Polymers/Polymers]] will have a range of chain lengths of differing molecular mass, or a mass distribution. Differences in molecular weight affect solubility, strength, viscosity among other properties.
## Strength
### Molecular Weight
When molecular weight is below a certain point, the polymer will have no mechanical strength. After this point, the higher the molecular weight, the higher the number of [[Chemistry/Intermolecular Forces/Intermolecular Forces]] (mainly [[Chemistry/Intermolecular Forces/London Dispersion Forces]]). The more [[Chemistry/Intermolecular Forces/Intermolecular Forces]], the higher that the strength will be. The molecular weight will increase with molecular strength up to a certain point, however it will reach a asymptotic eventually
### Molecular Shape
>[!Note]
>In order to compare the shape, they must both have approximately the same molecular weight

The other factor that will effect the strength of the polymers will be the surface area of the polymer. Because a linear polymer has a higher proportion of surface area, it will have higher [[Chemistry/Intermolecular Forces/Intermolecular Forces]].
