This is the addition of monomers without the loss of [[Chemistry/Polymers/Polymerization/Condensation Polymer/Condensates]] like a [[Chemistry/Polymers/Polymerization/Condensation Polymer/Condensation Polymer]]. We can identify this by a carbon backbone. 
For this, we keep the same bonding framework between the carbons. The difference is that there is initially a double bond between the two carbons, that then turns into the bonds between the monomers. We identify the [[Chemistry/Polymers/Polymerization/R-Groups]] located around the double bond and then place these back on the [[Chemistry/Polymers/Polymers]] afterwards. 
>[!Note]
>The symbol "R$\bullet$" shows that the compound is a radical.

In this form of bonding, the [[Radical Species]] kick-starts the polymerization, as the [[Radical Species]] forms a [[Chemistry/Atomic Structure and Properties/Bond/Bond]] with another electron present in the double bond of the carbon backbone. Then, the missing electron gets removed from the other end of the polymer, creating a new [[Radical Species]], and this will form another [[Chemistry/Atomic Structure and Properties/Bond/Bond]], and this chain reaction will repeat unit there is a lack of monomers to add on, or until it has another free [[Radical Species]] that adds onto the end.
It is important to have a low quantity of $R\bullet$ because if there are too many, the length of the [[Chemistry/Polymers/Polymers]] would be cut short. Also, the [[Radical Species]] will then begin to react with each other, and this causes a upper limit on the amount of useful radical species that can be added. 
## Steps of addition Polymerization
### Initiation
This is when the bonding just begins, and it is started off by a free radical species. These free radicals can be made by exposing specific chemicals to light or heat, breaking it and having the two portions become radicals which will start the reaction.
### Propagation
This is when the polymer chain will grow by adding on more monomers that are in the solution via addition polymerization
### Termination
This is when it occurs another free radical species, or when there is no more monomers to react with
