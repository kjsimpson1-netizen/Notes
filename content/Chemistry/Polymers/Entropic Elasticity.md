## Glass Transition Temperature
This is the temperature at which the polymer will switch from a hard, brittle state to a soft, pliable state. The higher that the glass transition temperature is, the more likely that the polymer will be stiff at room temperature, and vice versa with a lower glass transition temperature. 
The lower temperatures will have a higher stiffness and a lower flexibility because the polymer has a more crystalline structure, therefore it will be stiffer. The opposite is true when it is at high temperatures, because the molecules are moved around into a less crystalline structure, they become more pliable. 
>[!Note]
>This is not a phase change like melting or freezing, rather it is just the structure of the polymer linkages changing. There are no bonds being broken causing the molecule to change states, just the formation of the polymers themselves will be changed
