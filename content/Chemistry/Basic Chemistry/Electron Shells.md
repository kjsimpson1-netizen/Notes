Electrons in an element are organized into shells and subshells, based upon the quantity of electrons.
## How to write the Electron Configuration of an Element
As the subshells get higher in number, the atoms Potential Energy increases as well, and therefore it is harder for the electrons to stay there. To write these:
1. Write the number of the orbital (Which Period of the [[Periodic Table]] it belongs to)
2. Write the orbital letter (s,p,d,f)
3. Write as a power the number of electrons contained inside of that orbital (s-orbital has a maximum of 2, the p-orbital has a maximum of 6, the d-orbital has a maximum of 10, and the f orbital has a maximum of 14). 
4. Repeat this process in a list with all the electrons contained in the element
>[!Note]
>For the elements further down the table, the electron configuration can be written by first writing the preceeding noble gas, then adding on the excess electrons in the format mentioned above
>

>[!Warning]
>The 3d orbitals are filled before the 4s ones because they have a lower energy requirement and therefore will fill first
### Example 1
The element Nitrogen would be expressed as either:
$1s^2,2s^2,2p^3$
OR
$[\text{He}],2x^2,2p^3$
