This is the same idea as [[Partial Molar Volume]], and is expressed by the following equation:
$
\begin{aligned}
&\sum\limits_{i=1} \mu_i\Delta n_i\\
\hline\\
\mu&= \text{Potential Molar Energy}\\
n&= \text{Molar Quantity}
\end{aligned}
$
In order for this reaction to have [[Chemistry/Entropy/Spontaneity]], this must be equal to or less than 0. In order for this to be true, we must move from a higher to a lower $\mu$, or a decrease in chemical potential energy.
>[!Note]
>$\mu$ is a thermodynamic force and it depends upon the temperature and pressure of the system. 

$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_i&= \begin{cases}
PT\ln\left(\frac{P_i}{P_o}\right)&\text{ Ideal Gasses}\\
RT\ln(x_i)&\text{ Ideal Gasses, Solutions}\\
RT\ln\left(\frac{[i]}{M^o}\right)&\text{ Ideal Gasses, Solutions}\\
\tilde {V_i}P&\text{ Pure Condensed Phases}
\end{cases}\\
\hline\\
P&= \text{Pressure}\\
T&= \text{Temperature}\\
x_i&= \text{Mole Fraction}\\
i&= \text{Molar Concentration}\\
R&= \text{Gas Constant}\\
M&= \text{Standard Molarity}
\end{aligned}
$
The chemical will always choose the state with the lowest chemical potential energy.
>[!Note]
>This is the same as [[Chemistry/Entropy/Gibb's Free Energy]], however at a partial molar amount
