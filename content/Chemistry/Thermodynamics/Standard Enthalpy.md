This is [[Enthalpy]] in a reaction where 1 mole of substance is **formed** from elements int their standard states. This is represented by the symbol $\Delta{ H^o}_{f,T}$. Standard state means a gas at 1 bar, a pure liquid or solid in it's defined state, and for aqueous solutions at 1 molar. 
This is different than [[Chemistry/Basic Chemistry/STP]], as standard enthalpy states specific pressures and concentrations, however not temperatures. This means that the standard enthalpy of formation changes if the temperature changes. This means that the temperature of the environment must also be reported. 
>[!Note]
>$\Delta{ H^o}_{f,T}$ is 0 for all elements that are in standard state. 

## Formal Equation
$
$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta{ H^o}&= \sum\limits v_p\Delta{ H^o}(\text{reactants})-\sum\limits v_p\Delta{ H^o}(\text{products})\\
\hline\\
v_p&= \text{Stoicheometric Coefficient}\\
\Delta H^o&= \text{Standard Enthalpy of Formation}
\end{aligned}
$
$
We can also think of the products term as the bonds that are being formed, and the reactants are the bonds being broken,
