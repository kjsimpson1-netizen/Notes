This is the quantity of electron pairs that are in a [[Bond]]. For example, a [[Bond]] with a bond order of 2 has two electron pairs bonded there. The higher the bond order, the shorter and the more stiff the bond will be overall, and the lower the bond order, the longer and more loose the bond will be. 
## Resonance Structure
In any compound with a [[Physics/Electricity and Magnetism/Electric Circuits/Types of Circuits/Alternating Current/Resonance|Resonance]], there will be an average bond order for the average of all of the reasonable resonances. For example, benzene will have a bond order of 1.5, as there are only 2 [[Physics/Electricity and Magnetism/Electric Circuits/Types of Circuits/Alternating Current/Resonance|Resonance]] structures, one with a bond order of 1 and the other with a bond order of 2. We can also represent this with an equation:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Bond Order}&= \frac{\sum\text{Bond Orders}}{\text{Resonances}}
\end{aligned}
$$
