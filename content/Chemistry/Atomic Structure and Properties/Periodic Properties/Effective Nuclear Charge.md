Core electrons are close to the nucleus and will effectively screen some of it's positive charge from [[Chemistry/Basic Chemistry/Valence Electrons]] farther away. Valence electrons also partially screen each other from the nuclear charge. Thus, [[Chemistry/Basic Chemistry/Valence Electrons]] "feel" an effective nuclear charge ($Z_{eff}$) that is less than the actual nuclear charge, (Z). 
>[!Note]
>$Z_\text{eff}$ if a theoretically calculated property. It is calculated by $\text{Protons}-\text{Electrons}=Z_\text{eff}$. This is how many protons that the electrons "see". However, we must keep in mind that electrons that are in higher n-shells will shield less effectively than the electrons closer to the nucleus. 

## How it changes
### Across a Period
If we go across a period, then we can see than there is the same amount of core electrons, however the amount of protons in the elements will increase. This means that the $Z_\text{eff}$ will increase as we go left to right across the periods. 
### Down a group
If we go down a group, then the amount of core electrons and the amount of protons both increase, however the larger nuclear charge has a greater effect than the increased [[Atomic Radius]], so the overall effective nuclear charge will increase slightly as we move down a group. 
### Isoelectronic Species
Isoelectronic species are any elements that contain the same number of electrons in the same electron configuration. Because these species have the same number of electrons and therefore experience the same electron screening, and only the number of protons changes, the species with more protons will have a comparatively smaller [[Atomic Radius]], as there are  larger [[Chemistry/Coulomb Forces]], with the same electron repulsion (a higher effective nuclear charge).
