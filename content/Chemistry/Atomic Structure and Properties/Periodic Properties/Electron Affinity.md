This is the energy change resulting from the addition of a single electron to an atom or a molecule in it's gaseous state. This is an exothermic process, meaning that it releases energy and [[Chemistry/Thermodynamics/Enthalpy]] has a negative value. This means a larger electron affinity will have a more negative [[Chemistry/Thermodynamics/Enthalpy]]. The element releases energy because as it has an electron added to it, it becomes more stable and this change from a less stable to a more stable state releases the difference in Potential Energy. 
If we try to add another electron to an element, then it can either be accepted or rejected depending on if the reaction is energetically favourable. To be energetically favourable, the species with an electron has to be at a more stable state than the species without it. This only works with specific elements, as most do not want to accept and extra electron.
$$
$$\begin{aligned}
X(g)+e^-\rightarrow X^-(g)+H
\end{aligned}$$
$$
### Noble Gas Configurations
Nobel gas electron configurations are especially stable so atoms or ions with such a configuration are energetically favourable. This means that $Na^+$ is more energetically favourable than $Na$ due to this reason
>[!Warning]
>This excludes the noble gasses as it is unfavourable to add another electron to them
## How it changes?
### Across a period
If we go across a period from left to right, the [[Atomic Radius]] will decrease and therefore there is more energy released. This means that the electron affinity increases. 
### Down a group
As we go down a group, the [[Atomic Radius]] will increase, and therefore there is less energy released as. This means that the electron affinity will go down as we go down the group.
In general, the elements in the upper right of the [[Chemistry/Basic Chemistry/Periodic Table]] have the highest electron affinity.
