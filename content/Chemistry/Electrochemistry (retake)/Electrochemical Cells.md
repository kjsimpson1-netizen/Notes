These are also called voltaic cells, Daniel cells and galvanic cells and they run on Oxidation Reduction reactions. 
Look for the stronger of the oxidization agents (higher on the chart), and this will be the compound that will gain the electrons and this is the direction that the electrons will flow. 
In the copper-zinc batteries, the copper is stronger therefore it will gain the electrons following this reaction:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
Cu^{+2}+2e^-\rightarrow Cu_{(s)}
\end{aligned}
$
$
The zinc is weaker, so it will give up the electrons following this reaction:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
Zn_{(s)}\rightarrow Zn^{+2}+2e^-
\end{aligned}
$
$
## Galvanic Notation
We can also express this cell using galvanic notation:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Anode }|\text{ Anode Electrolyte }||\text{ Cathode Electrolyte }|\text{ Cathode}
\end{aligned}
$
$
The side that is positive is the end of the battery that the electrons flow from, and the negative side is the side of the battery that the electrons will flow towards. The positive side is called the anode, and the negative side is called the cathode. The battery "dies" when the anode is dissolved and used up. 
The solutions in the two cells are called electrolytes. 
>[!Note]
>The reduction reaction goes on the right hand side of the cell and the oxidation goes on the left hand side
## The Salt Bridge
This is to allow the flow of charge between the two half cells, so that the overall charge b=of the battery will remain 0. The electrolytes in the salt bridge are 2 very weak electrolytes, so that they do not want to gain or lose charge. 

The positive ions are the cations, and these will want to go to the cathode, and the negative ions, the anions are attracted to the anode. 

The side that is higher on the chart on the oxidizing agent side will be the cathode side. 

The solid of the anode will decrease in mass and the electrolytes of the anode will increase in concentration. The solid of the cathode will increase in mass and the electrolytes of the anode will decrease in current. 
## Overall Charge
The overall charge of the cell is given by the difference between the reduction reaction and the oxidation reaction:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
E_\text{Cell}&= E_R-E_L\\
\hline\\
E_\text{Cell}&= \text{Stanard Cell Potential}\\
E_\text{R}&= \text{Right Cell Potential}\\
E_\text{L}&= \text{Left Cell Potential}
\end{aligned}
$
$
>[!Warning]
>The electrical cell potential is an **INTENSIVE PROPERTY** and does not get multiplied when balancing
