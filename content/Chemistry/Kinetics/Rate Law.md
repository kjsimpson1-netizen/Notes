This is the rate law of which the speed at which the reaction will progress. This is expressed by the following:
$$
$$\begin{aligned}
\text{Rate}&= k[A]^n[B]^m\\
\hline\\
k&= \text{Rate Constant}
\end{aligned}$$
$$
Where the variable $m$ and $n$ are the [[Reaction Order]]. 
>[!Note]
>If a reactant is in an extreme excess, then it can be ignored in the rate law
## 0th Order
When rearranged into a slope-intercept form, this will give us the form:
$$
$$\begin{aligned}
[A]_t&= -kt+[A]_0
\end{aligned}$$
$$
This means that if you plot the concentrations versus time and you get a straight line, this means that it is a 0th order
## 1st Order
When rearranged into a slope-intercept form, this will give us the form:
$$
$$\begin{aligned}
\ln([A]_t) &= -kt+\ln([A]_0)
\end{aligned}$$
$$
This means that if you plot the natural logarithm of the concentrations, it will give you a straight line if it is 1st order
## 2nd Order
When rearranged into a slope-intercept form, this will give us the form:
$$
$$\begin{aligned}
\frac1{[A]_t}&= kt+\frac1{[A]_0}
\end{aligned}$$
$$
This is the same idea as above, with the reciprocals of the concentrations plotted with respect to time giving us a straight line, telling us that it is a second order function.
