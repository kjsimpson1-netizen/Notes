This is the equation for the exponential change of the reaction. It takes the form of any exponential growth or decay:
$
\begin{aligned}
k&= Ae^{\frac{E_a}{RT}}\\
\hline\\
k&= \text{Rate Constant}\\
A&= \text{Frequency Factor}\\
E_a&= \text{Activation Energy}\\
R&= \text{Gas Constant}\\
T&= \text{Temperature}
\end{aligned}
$
This is used to relate the [[Rate Constant]] or the [[Rate Law]] to the activation energy and the temperature. 
## Linear Form
We can also rearrange this into a linear form to allow us to graph it to find the $E_a$ and the $k$:
$
\begin{aligned}
\ln(k)&= -\frac{E_a}R\cdot\frac1T+\ln A\\
\hline\\
k&= \text{Rate Constant}\\
A&= \text{Pre-exponential Factor}\\
E_a&= \text{Activation Energy}\\
R&= \text{Gas Constant}\\
T&= \text{Temperature}
\end{aligned}
$
For this graph, the y-intercept would be the natural logarithm of the frequency factor and the slope would be the negative [[Activation Energy]] divided by the gas constant.
