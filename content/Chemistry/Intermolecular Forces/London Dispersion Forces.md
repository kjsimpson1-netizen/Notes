These exist in **every** single species that has electrons. This is caused by the fact that the electrons are not bound on orbits, and they can be randomly collected on one portion of the molecule, causing a small, transient dipole. This small negative dipole will cause the neighbouring atoms to have a small positive dipole, and then this chain reaction causes the London dispersion forces. 
The things that make these forces stronger are the quantity of electrons (the more electrons that stronger), as well have a larger [[Chemistry/Atomic Structure and Properties/Periodic Properties/Atomic Radius]], as the [[Chemistry/Basic Chemistry/Valence Electrons]] are more free to move around, and can be influenced more easily. The other thing that effects it is the molecule being branched versus linear, the linear will have a higher polarizability, because there is more electron reaction surface area with linear molecules.
These two effects are called **polarizability**, the more London dispersion forces that the molecule feels, the more polarizable that the molecule is. This means that even non polar molecules can have small dipoles. 
If these forces increase, we can see this effect macroscopically by the raising of the boiling point of the [[Chemistry/Atomic Structure and Properties/Bond/Covalent Compounds]].
