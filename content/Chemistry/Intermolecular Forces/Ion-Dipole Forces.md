Also known as charge-dipole forces, these are very similar to [[Dipole-Dipole Forces]] but with an ion in place of one of the dipoles, and this can be thought of as the "solvation" of ions. When table salt is placed in water, it is dissolved into the water. The two ions, $Na^+$ and $Cl^-$, will be attracted to the oppositely charged dipole of the water molecule. This means that the two ions of table salt will dissociate. We can express this quantitatively as an equation:
$$
\begin{aligned}
E&\propto\frac{|z|\mu}{r^2}\\
\\
z&\text{ is the charge of the ion}\\
\mu&\text{ is the dipole moment}\\
r&\text{ is the distance}
\end{aligned}
$$
By looking at the equation, we can also see that the smaller the [[Chemistry/Atomic Structure and Properties/Periodic Properties/Atomic Radius]] and the larger the magnitude of the charge on the molecules, the larger the charge-dipole forces.
