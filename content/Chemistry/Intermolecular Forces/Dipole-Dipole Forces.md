Dipole-dipole forces are the forces that occur when there is a portion of [[Chemistry/Atomic Structure and Properties/Bond/Covalent Compounds]] that possess a higher [[Chemistry/Atomic Structure and Properties/Bond/Electron Density]], causing it to have a dipole. These dipole will then attract each other through [[Chemistry/Coulomb Forces]], causing dipole-dipole forces. 
These are also known as polar interactions. These are stronger than [[London Dispersion Forces]] forces. These molecules must have [[Chemistry/Atomic Structure and Properties/Bond/Polar Covalent Compounds]]. The partial positives of the dipoles are represented by $\delta^\pm$, depending if it is a positive or a negative dipole. 
## Drawing a Dipole
The line that is drawn on the perspective diagrams is drawn from positive dipole to negative dipole. The direction that this arrow points depends on which end has a partial positive and which end has a partial positive. These dipoles are a permanent feature of the [[Chemistry/Atomic Structure and Properties/Bond/Covalent Compounds]] that they are a part of.
