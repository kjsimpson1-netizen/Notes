### Features of the graph
#### Vapor Pressure
The line between the gas and the liquid represents the [[Vapor Pressure]], as whenever there is a liquid or solid that is on the interface with gas, there is a layer of vapor on top.
#### Triple Point
The triple point represents where all 3 states of matter can exist at the same point.
#### Critical Point
The critical point is very important while labelling these diagrams, as it tells us where the liquid and the gas must exist.  After the critical point, there is no longer a vapor pressure and therefore the meniscus does not exist as there is no defined difference between the two phases. The uniform fluid, or supercritical fluid, occurs when the critical point is surpassed. 
#### Phase Locations
The gas phase will be located at high temperature, low pressure.
The solid phase will be located at low temperature and high pressure. 
The liquid phase will be located somewhere in the middle. 
#### Interpreting the Graph
When there is a negative slope on the solid-liquid boundary line, this means that the solid is less dense than the liquid
## Density
Looking at the pressure temperature phase diagram, if we move in a direction that increases the pressure or decreases the temperature, the density of the substance will increase.
