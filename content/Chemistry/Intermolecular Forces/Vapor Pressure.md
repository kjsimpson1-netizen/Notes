This is the pressure that is caused from the vapor that exists on the top of a liquid.  The larger that the [[Intermolecular Forces]] are, the smaller the vapor pressure is. This is because less of the substance will contribute to the vapor as the bonds are stronger and therefore the substance will be more likely to stay in the liquid state.
## Total Pressure and Partial Pressure
There is total pressure (pressure of all the substances in the air) and partial pressure (the pressure of a specific molecule). A vapor pressure is a special type of partial pressure, and it occurs anywhere on the [[Pressure-Temperature Phase Diagram]] where there is a border between the gas and another phase. The molecules changing state from liquid or solid to gas is more likely for the substance on the surface, as there are less bonds between the molecules and therefore the [[Intermolecular Forces]] are weaker. We can also get the boiling point of this by looking and the [[Pressure-Temperature Phase Diagram]], by looking at the specific pressure that the substance is at, we can find the corresponding temperature and this would be the boiling temperature. We can get the total pressure by summing up all the partial pressures (vapor pressures) at that specific temperature. 
 >[!Note 1]
 >It is very important that the system be at thermal equilibrium.
 
 >[!Note 2]
>At the boiling point, the vapour pressure is equal to atmospheric pressure.
