Rolle's Theorem guarantees the existence of a point where the derivative is zero when a function has equal values at two endpoints.
## Theorem
Let $f\in C^1([a,b])$ and suppose
$$
f(a)=f(b)=0
$$
Then there exists some $\zeta\in(a,b)$ such that
$$
f'(\zeta)=0
$$
More generally, if
$$
f(a)=f(b)
$$
then there exists $\zeta\in(a,b)$ such that
$$
f'(\zeta)=0
$$
## Connection to Interpolation Error
Rolle's Theorem is used repeatedly when deriving interpolation and Taylor polynomial error formulas. It allows us to prove that certain derivatives must vanish at intermediate points.
This provides the theoretical foundation for results such as the [[Taylor Polynomial Error]] and interpolation error formulas.
