Exponential growth and decay are both examples of [[Math/Calculus/Derivative/Differential Equations/Differential Equations]], and examples of these are population growth, radioactive decay, and the sharing of social media. The defining part of an exponential growth or decay is that the rate, or the [[Derivative]], is dependant upon the quantity. This is expressed by:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dy}{dx}&= k\cdot y\\
\hline\\
k&= \text{Growth Constant}\\
y&= \text{Initial Value}
\end{aligned}
$$
>[!Note]
>If it is an exponential decay, then there will be a negative growth constant. 

All of the solutions to this equation take the form:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{dy}{dt}&= A\cdot ke^{kt}=k\cdot y
\end{aligned}
$$
