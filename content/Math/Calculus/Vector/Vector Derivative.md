These are very similar to derivatives with regular functions, which denote the direction of the [[Tangent Line]] of the function at the given point. For vectors, this is exactly what it does. We can see this from the [[Derivative Definition]]:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac d{dt}\vec r(t)=\lim_{h\rightarrow 0}\frac{\vec r(t+h)-\vec r(t)}{h}
\end{aligned}
$$
This shows us that the difference between the two heads of the vectors will be the vector derivative; hence, the direction of the tangent line pointing in the direction of increasing $t$.
We can also take the [[Derivative|Derivatives]] of vector based function. For example, where :$$\vec r(t)= \begin{bmatrix}
x(t)\\y(t)
\end{bmatrix}$$
The [[Vector]] derivative of this will be:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{d}{dt}\vec r(t)=\begin{bmatrix}
x'(t)\\
y'(t)
\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$
In fact, [[Derivative Rules]] all apply to vectors as well. However, we must be careful to differentiate between [[Dot Product]] and [[Cross Product]]. Also when taking the [[Cross Product]], ensure that the order is kept in the [[Product Rule]]
