An improper [[Integral]] occurs when there is a source of impropriety, being:
1. There is a [[Discontinuity]] (a [[Singular Point]]) occurring in the [[Bounds]] of the integrand, or 
2. When the [[Bounds]] are to [[Infinity]]. 
However, these improper integrals can be evaluated if they converge quickly enough. Both of these cases can be evaluated using the [[Cauchy Principle Value]]. Once the [[Integral]] has had the issue delt with, it can be treated as a regular [[Definite Integral]], evaluating with the [[Bounds]] at the end. 
>[!Note]
>The values at [[Infinity]] may have to be evaluated with [[Horizontal Asymptotes]] or [[L'Hopitals Rule]]
