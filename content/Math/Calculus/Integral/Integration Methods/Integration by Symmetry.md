If the [[Bounds]] are symmetrical and it has a [[Symmetrical Functions|Symmetrical Function]] , then we can take a shortcut:
### Even Function
Given:
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
A&= \int_{-a}^af(x)dx=2\int_0^af(x)dx
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
With $f(x)$ being an even function, this will cause the same area to accumulate on either side of $x=0$. This means we can just take half the bound, and double the area
### Odd Function
Given:
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
A&= \int_{-a}^af(x)dx=0
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
With $f(x)$ being an odd function, the area will be 0 since the positive and negative will completely cancel out
>[!Note]
>This applies to [[Multivariable]] calculus as well, if either of the variables has a symmetrical set of bounds and it is an odd or even function with respect to that variable, we can then use the same logic
