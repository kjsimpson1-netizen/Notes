Inverse functions are when the $x$ and $y$ values are switched around, so the output of $f(x)$ is equal to the input of $f(y)$ necessary to get the original answer. The range, domain, $x$ and $y-value$s of the function and it's inverse also interchange. The graphs are mirrored across the line $y=x$, therefore, the [[Derivative]] is the reciprocal as well. 

The shortcut for these is "flip/flip/flip". The three things that get flipped are:
1. The other function
2. The reciprocal
3. At the other point
## Function
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\\
\text{For the point }&(a,b)\\
\frac d{dx}f^{-1}(x)\Big|_{x=b}&= \frac 1{f'(f^{-1}(b))}=\frac1{f'(a)}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
## Example
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x)&= x^2,\;x\ge0\\\\

f^{-1}(x)&=\sqrt x\\\\

\frac d{dx}f(x)&= 2x\\
\\
\frac d{dx}f^{-1}(x)&= \frac 1{2(\sqrt x)}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
