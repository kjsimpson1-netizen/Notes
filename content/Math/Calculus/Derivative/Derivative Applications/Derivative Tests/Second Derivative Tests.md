These are used to determine the concavity of graphs, as well as the behaviour of functions. The second [[Derivative]] can tell us about the acceleration of a moving object as a function of time, or the rate at which a rate is changing. 
## Concavity
For graphing, it can also tell us what the graph will look like. If the second derivative is positive, this means the function is concave up on that portion, and will look like an upright cup or bowl, and vice versa for negative.
## Extrema
If the second derivative is negative at [[Critical Points]], then these [[Critical Points]] are local maxima, and vice versa for local minima. This is not overly useful though, as it doesn't tell us anything if the second derivative is 0, and it also doesn't tell us if it is a global or local.
