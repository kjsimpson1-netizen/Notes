 This is the same idea as [[Cylindrical Integration]], however now instead of having a height that is not dependent upon the radius (a sphere), it now looks more like a sphere. This takes the form:
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\iiint_E(\rho^2\sin(\phi))d\rho\;d\phi\;d\theta
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
We can also replace any instances of $x$, $y$, and $z$ as:
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
(x,y,z)=\begin{cases}
x = \rho \sin\varphi \cos\theta, \\
y = \rho \sin\varphi \sin\theta, \\
z = \rho \cos\varphi.
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
