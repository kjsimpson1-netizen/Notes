These are the x-values where the function is equal to 0. For any nth degree polynomial, there are $n$ roots. Even though there may be no [[Real Numbers]] roots, there can still be [[Complex Numbers]] roots. For example, even though $\sqrt1$ only has one real root, there is another root in the [[Complex Numbers]], which can be found using [[Euler's Method]], or the [[Quadratic Equation]]. 
>[!Note]
>There can be "repeated roots" which are when there are multiple roots that all occur at the same point. There are multiple roots here, however they overlap. In other words, the algebraic multiplicity is higher than the geometric multiplicity.
