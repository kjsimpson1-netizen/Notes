## Power Functions
A graph of the [[Power Function]]:
$f(x)=x$, $f(x)=x^2$, $f(x)=x^3$, and $f(x)=x^4$
![[ComparingFunctions.png|600]]
From looking at this graph, we can see that:
When $x$ is big, 
$x\ll x^2\ll x^3\ll x^4$

When x is small,
$x\gg x^2\gg x^3\gg x^4$

This basically just means that the higher the degree of the variable, the smaller it will be closer to 0, and the larger it will be as the variable gets very large.
##### $f(x)=x^2+x^5+x^7$
From looking at the [[Power Function]] and looking at the [[Dominant Terms]], we can infer as to what the graph of the function will look like:
$$
\begin{aligned}
x^2+x^5+x^7\approx\begin{cases}
\text{when }x\text{ is small, }\approx x^2\\
\text{when }x\text{ is large, }\approx x^7\\
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
## Rational Functions
The same concepts that worked for comparing the [[Power Function]] will also work for any [[Rational Functions]], just using the same principle over the numerator and the denominator separately. 
### $\frac{5x^2}{3x^3-7}$
We can express this using a [[Piecewise Function]]:
$$
\begin{aligned}
f(x)&\approx \begin{cases}
-\frac{5x^2}{7},&\text{When x is small}\\
\frac{5x^2}{3x^3},&\text{When x is large}
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
## Exponential Functions
While comparing [[Exponential Functions]], we must keep in mind that at one extremity, they approach 0, and at the other it will approach [[Infinity]]. This means that they will always dominate at one extreme and will always be dominated at the other. 
## Logarithmic Functions
[[Logarithmic Functions]] are much like [[Exponential Functions]], as in they have one end that is extremely dominant and the other extremity is less so. 
### $\log(x)$ and $e^x$
#### Comparing $\log x$ and x
$\log x$ versus $x^\frac14$ :

$\log x<x^\frac14$ for:    $0<x<4.177$ 
$\log x>x^\frac14$ for:   $4.177<x<5503.66$
$\log x<x^\frac14$ for:   $x>5503.66$
