This is an [[Ordinary Differential Equations|ODE]] where there is not just a first [[Order]] [[Derivative]], but also a second order [[Derivative]] as well. These will take the form:
$$
\begin{aligned}
y''(x)+p(x)y'(x)+q(x)y&= 0
\end{aligned}
$$
>[!Theorem 1]
>If $y_1(x)$ and $y_2(x)$ are both solutions to a second [[Order]] [[Linearity|Linear Equation]], then any [[Linear Combinations]] of the two will also be a [[Solutions]]. Therefore, their general solution will take the form:
>$$
\begin{aligned}
y(x)=C_1y_1(x)+C_2y_2(x)\qquad C_1,C_2\in\mathbb{R}
\end{aligned}
$$

>[!Theorem 2]
>If $p(x)$ and $q(x)$ are both continuous functions on the interval that contains $x_0$, then the [[Solutions]] will have a unique solution .
