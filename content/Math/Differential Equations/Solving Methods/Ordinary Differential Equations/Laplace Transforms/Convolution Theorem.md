This is useful when it comes to [[Laplace Transforms]], as it gives us another way to find [[Inverse Laplace Transforms]]. This can be written as:
$$
$$\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{L}\Big\{(f*g)(t)\Big\}(s)&= F(s)\cdot G(s)\\
\mathcal{L}^{-1}\Big\{F(s)\cdot G(s)\Big\}(t)&= (f*g)(t)
\end{aligned}$$
$$
This is especially useful when we can find a product of two [[Inverse Laplace Transforms]], which we can use [[Convolution]] instead of [[Partial Fractions]]
