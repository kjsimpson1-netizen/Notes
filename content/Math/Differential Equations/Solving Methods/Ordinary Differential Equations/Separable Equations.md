An [[Ordinary Differential Equations|ODE]] is separable if we can write it as:
$$
$$\begin{aligned}
y'(x)&= f(x)g(y)
\end{aligned}$$and separate the independent variables on either side of the equation which will allow us to take the [[Antiderivative]] of both sides. For some functions $f(x)$ and $g(y)$. We can then shuffle this around:
$$
$$
$$\begin{aligned}
\int\frac{dy}{g(y)}&= \int f(x)dx+C
\end{aligned}$$
$$
This can then be integrated and solved for the independent variable. This will often include exponentials and logarithms, as the antiderivative of a reciprocal is the natural logarithm. 
>[!Note]
>We don't actually move the $dx$ around, rather we use the substitution rule which effectively has the same outcome as multiplying by $dx$
