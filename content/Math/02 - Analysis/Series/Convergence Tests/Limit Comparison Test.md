## Integrals
This test states that, letting:$$
\begin{aligned}
F=\int_a^\infty f(x)dx\quad G=\int_a^\infty g(x)dx
\end{aligned}
$$
Where $G$ here is the [[Comparison Function]], and if:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{x\rightarrow \infty}\frac{f(x)}{g(x)}=L,\qquad L\in(0,\infty)
\end{aligned}
$$
Then the behaviour of $F$ matches that of $G$ and vice versa
### Choosing $f(x)$ and $g(x)$
In any given question, $f(x)$ will be the simplified version of the integrand, and $g(x)$ will be a benchmark function which the behavior of is well known and is easy to compare it to. 
## Series
This is the same principle as for integrals. For any series $\sum a_n$ and $\sum b_n$, if the limit of their ratio exists, then they share the convergence behaviour.
$$
\begin{aligned}
L=\lim_{n\rightarrow \infty}\frac{a_n}{b_n}\qquad L\in(1,\infty)
\end{aligned}
$$
