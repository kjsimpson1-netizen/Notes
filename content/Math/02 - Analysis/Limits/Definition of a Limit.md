The definition of a [[Limit]] is:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{x\rightarrow a}f(x)=L
\end{aligned}
$$
We can read this as: The [[Limit]] of $f(x)$ as x approaches a is L. This means that the Function, $f(x)$, can be made arbitrary close to L, when $x$ is close to a (but not exactly equal to a). We don't care what the function does exactly at $a$, we just care about what it does as it gets really really close to a. This is important because if the function is not defined at this point, we don't care what it actual is for limits, just what it is close to.
>[!Note]
>In order for the limit to exist, it must only approach one value and both [[One Sided Limits]] must be the same:
>$$
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{x\rightarrow a^+}f(x)=\lim_{x\rightarrow a^-}f(x)
\end{aligned}
$$
