These are [[Math/Analysis/Series/Series]] where the next term cancels the previous one, and therefore as the sum continues on to infinity, only certain values don't get cancel and therefore the series will converge even though the [[Sequences]] may not go to infinity. An example of this is:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{n=1}^\infty\left(\frac1n+\frac1{n+1}\right)
\end{aligned}
$
$
From this we can see that the first few terms:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
\sum_{n=1}^\infty \left( \frac{1}{n} - \frac{1}{n+1} \right)
= \left( \frac{1}{1} - \frac{1}{2} \right)
+ \left( \frac{1}{2} - \frac{1}{3} \right)
+ \left( \frac{1}{3} - \frac{1}{4} \right)
+ \left( \frac{1}{4} - \frac{1}{5} \right)
+ \cdots
\end{aligned}
$
$
From this, each term other then 1 will cancel, and therefore the series will converge to 1 as $n\rightarrow\infty$.
