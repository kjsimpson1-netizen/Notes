A graph with continuity is one that has no breaks or jumps on it (it can be drawn without lifting your pencil). Polynomials, rational functions, trigonometry are all continuous where they are defined.
## Continuity at a point
If, $\lim_{x\rightarrow a}f(x)= f(a)$ then $f(x)$ is continuous at $f(a)$. This means that there is no [[Discontinuity]] in the function and there is no "hole" in the function.
## Continuity as a function
The function is continuous at $x=a$ if:
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
\lim_{x\rightarrow a^-}f(x)&= f(a)=\lim_{x\rightarrow a^+}f(x)
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
This just shows that each of the [[One Sided Limits]] are equivalent to the function evaluated at the same value, $x=a$.
