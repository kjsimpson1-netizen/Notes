This is an equation to describe a line in any dimensional space.
In the following, let $\vec p=(p_1,p_2)$ be the point and $\vec l=(l_1,l_2)$ be the direction. 
$
$
\begin{aligned}
\boxed{\vec x=t\vec l+\vec p}
\end{aligned}
$
$
We can say that $\vec x$ is equal to the [[Vector]] $l$ extended indefinitely from the point $p$.
## Expansion
The parametric form of a line can be expanded into a single vector by adding together the point and the [[Vector]]:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
\vec x&= t\vec l+\vec p\\
&= t \begin{bmatrix}
l_1\\
l_2\\
l_3
\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}
p_1\\
p_2\\
p_3
\end{bmatrix}\\
&= \begin{bmatrix}
tl_1\\
tl_2\\
tl_3
\end{bmatrix}+\begin{bmatrix}
p_1\\
p_2\\
p_3
\end{bmatrix}\\
&= \begin{bmatrix}
tl_1+p_1\\
tl_2+p_2\\
tl_3+p_3
\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$
$
>[!Note]
>If in the expanded form, the scalar $t$ doesn't appear in a row, this means that the line has no component in that direction i.e. it is parallel to that direction
## Multiple Equations
The expanded form can also be rewritten as multiple equations for different dimensions. For example:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
\vec x&= \begin{bmatrix}
tl_1+p_1\\
tl_2+p_2\\
tl_3+p_3
\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$
$
Can be rewritten as:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
 \begin{cases}
x&= tl_1+p_1\\
y&= tl_2+p_2\\
z&= tl_3+p_3
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$
$
