This is when [[Planes]] are expressed as the components with coefficients, set equal to a constant:
$
\begin{aligned}
Ax+By+Cz&= D
\end{aligned}
$
This form is useful for finding what points satisfy the plane, as any points $(x,y,z)$ that satisfy the equation will be a point on the plane.
## Translating from [[Point-Normal Formula]]
It is very easy to go from the [[Point-Normal Formula]] to the scalar form of a plane. We can take the equation for the [[Point-Normal Formula]] and expand it out, with both vectors in $\mathbb{R}^3$:
>[!Note]
>The [[Dot Product]] of two vectors is a scalar, hence the $D$ being the product of $\vec n\cdot \vec p$
$
\begin{aligned}
\vec n\cdot \vec x&= \vec n\cdot \vec p\\
n_xx+n_yy+n_zz&= D
\end{aligned}
$
