This is a [[Matrix]] which takes the form shown below, aptly named a lower triangular matrix due to it's shape.
$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}
*&0&0\\
*&*&0\\
*&*&*
\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$
>[!Note]
>This is called a **unit** triangular matrix if that diagonals are all 1:
>$$
\begin{aligned}
\begin{bmatrix}
1&0&0\\
*&1&0\\
*&*&1
\end{bmatrix}
\end{aligned}
$$
