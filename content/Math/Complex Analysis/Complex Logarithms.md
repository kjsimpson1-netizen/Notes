For logarithms of [[Complex Numbers]], they are defined on $\mathbb{C}\setminus (-\infty,0]$. The logarithm of a complex number is most easily taken in [[Polar Complex Numbers]], and this will give an output of:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\text{Log}(re^{i\theta})=\log r+i\theta
\end{aligned}
$$
Essentially extracting the angle. 
>[!Note]
>The capital "L" in $\text{Log}$ denotes that this takes the principle angle of the [[Polar Complex Numbers]].
***

Following the [[Derivative]] of a regular logarithm, we have that:
$$
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\partial }{\partial z}\log(z)=\frac1z
\end{aligned}
$$
## Properties of $\log(z)$
$$
\begin{aligned}
z&= e^{\log(z)}&&(1)\\
z&\neq \log(e^z)&&(2)\\
\log(z_1z_2)&\neq \log(z_1)+\log(z_2)&&(3)
\end{aligned}
$$
Where $(1)$ follows our usual logarithm rules, however $(2)$ and $(3)$ do not.
