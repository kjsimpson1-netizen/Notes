A **force** is a push or pull upon an object resulting from the object's _interaction_ with another object. Whenever there is an _interaction_ between two objects, there is a force upon each of the objects. When the _interaction_ ceases, the two objects no longer experience the force. Forces only exist as a result of an interaction
## Weight
This is the gravitational force on any object. This is equal to the mass of the object, multiplied by the acceleration due to gravity:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
\vec F&= mg
\end{aligned}
$
$
## Normal Force
This is any force that equally opposes another force. This is the same thing as the force that is exerted upwards by the floor to prevent you from falling through it. These are always perpendicular to the contact surface
## Friction Force
This is force that opposes the motion on surfaces. The coefficient of friction is $\mu$. There are two types of friction:
### Static Friction
This is when the friction force is equal to the force attempting to move the object. This is represented by $\vec F_s$. The static friction will always be equal to force it's opposing. In order for this to remain static, the following must be true:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
|\vec F_s|<\mu_sN
\end{aligned}
$
$
If this is true, then:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
\vec F_a&= -\vec F_s
\end{aligned}
$
$
### Dynamic Friction
This is the friction force that occurs when the object is in motion, and the friction again opposes the motion. However, the kinetic friction is always the same. 
$
$
\begin{aligned}
\vec F_d&= N\cdot\mu_d
\end{aligned}
$
$
>[!Note]
>The static coefficient of friction is often greater than the dynamic coefficient 
## Tension
This is the force exerted by ropes against whichever force is pulling against it. This is always equal to the magnitude of the force pulling against it. 
>[!Note]
>The force that the rope exerts one way on the object is equal but opposite to the force exerts on the other end following [[Newton's Third Law]]
## Elastic Force
This is the force of springs that follows [[Hooke's Law]].
