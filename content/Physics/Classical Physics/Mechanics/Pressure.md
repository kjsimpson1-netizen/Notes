Pressure is determined as $P=\frac FA$ , with the units of Pascals ($Pa$). For an ideal gas, the temperature is directly proportional to the pressure, and therefore this can be used as a method to determine the temperature of the gas, and therefore the environment around it once it is in [[Thermal Equilibrium]]. 
## Water cup example
In the cup and the cardboard example, the water doesn't fall out because the air pressure exerts a [[Force]] up on the cardboard. 
The two forces of the water pushing down and the atmospheric pressure pushing up are equal, and therefore can be represented by the following equation.

$$
\begin{aligned}
P&= \frac FA=\frac{mg}A\\
&= \frac{(\rho Ah)g}{A}\\
&= \rho hg
\end{aligned}
