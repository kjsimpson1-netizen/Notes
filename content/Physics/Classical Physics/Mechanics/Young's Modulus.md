This is the idea that all object are not truly rigid, and undergo squeezing and twisting as there is a [[Force]] that is applied. 
## Hooke's law
This is the ratio between the [[Stress]] applied and the resulting [[Strain]]:
$$
$$\begin{aligned}
\frac{\text{Stress}}{\text{Strain}}&= \text{Elastic Modulus}
\end{aligned}$$
$$
>[!Note]
This is called elastic because the object that is deformed returns to it's original size when the stress is removed. The value of the elastic modulus is due to the material that it is made of, not the size or the shape of the object. If an object is not elastic, then it is plastic.
### Hooke's Law
Hooke's law is only applicable to objects in the elastic range, and not the plastic range
![[StressVs.Strain.png]]
Research shows that for a sufficiently small tensile [[Stress]], [[Stress]] and [[Strain]] are proportional shown by the following equations, called the "Young's Modulus".
$$
$$\begin{aligned}
Y=\frac{\text{Tensile Stress}}{\text{Tensile Strain}}=\frac{\frac{F_\perp}{A}}{\frac{\Delta l}{l_0}}&= \frac{F_\perp l_0}{A\;\Delta l}
\end{aligned}$$
$$
![[YoungsModulusTable.png]]
There are no units for any of these, and they are just straight numerical values. The higher the value, the more stress to result in the same strain.
