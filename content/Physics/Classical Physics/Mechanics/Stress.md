This is the measure of amount of [[Force]] per unit area, represented by $\sigma$. This has the same units as pressure, in Pascals. There are 3 types of stress:
1. Tensile stress (guitar string)
2. Bulk stress (divers pressure)
3. Shear stress (scissors)
## Tensile 
This is the stress that is applied to springs and to ropes under tension. This is when there is an equal [[Force]] (it's important that it is equal so there is no net force and no acceleration following [[Newton's Second Law]]) on both sides of the object, trying to stretch it apart. The tensile stress can be defined with the following equation:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
\sigma&= \frac{F_\perp}{A}
\end{aligned}
$
$

>[!Note]
The subscript "$\perp$" is important because it indicates that the forces is perpendicular to the cross sectional area. This value is a scalar because $F_\perp$ is the magnitude, not the direction, of the [[Force]].
## Compression
This is much the same as tensile stress, but with the two ends being squished together and the $\Delta l$ being negative rather than positive. For many materials, the Young's modulus has the same value for both stress and [[Strain]], however some materials, such as composite materials (concrete and stone) can withstand much higher compressive stresses than tensile stresses. 
Objects can be placed under both compressive and tensile forces. For example, a steel beam has compressive forces at the top and tensile forces at the bottom. To deal with this, the top and bottom of the beam are given higher surface areas, while the middle is smaller, resulting in the shape of an I-beam, as the mid-line is neither under compressive nor tensile stress.
