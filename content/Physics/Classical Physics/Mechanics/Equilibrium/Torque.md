This is any [[Force]] at a distance, also called moments. These usually occur about pivot points, and the greater the length and the greater the [[Force]], the greater the torque. The units for torque are Newton-Metres, $Nm$. It is also dependant upon the angle at which you apply the force, and can be represented with:
>[!Note]
>The distance direction here is from the point of the moment to the axis
$$
\begin{aligned}
\vec\tau&= \vec r\times \vec F\\
\hline\\
\tau &= \text{Torque}\\
F&= \text{Force}\\
r&= \text{Distance}
\end{aligned}
$$
>[!Note]
>The magnitude of this force is given by:
>$$
\begin{aligned}
||\vec\tau||&= Fd\sin\theta
\end{aligned}
$$

If the cross product of the torque is positive, then the moment is clockwise because the cross is in the positive $z$-direction. The opposite is true with a negative direction with respect to the $z$ axis being counter-clockwise.
