This is the [[Simple Harmonic Motion]] for a rotating object. We can represent the [[Torque]] with the expression:
$
\begin{aligned}
\tau&= -\kappa\theta\\
\hline\\
\tau&= \text{Torque}\\
\kappa&= \text{Tortion Constant}\\
\theta&= \text{Angular Displacement}
\end{aligned}
$
## Angular Frequency
We can relate these to angular frequency with the [[Moment of Inertia]] and the torsion constant:
$
\begin{aligned}
\omega&= \sqrt\frac\kappa I\\
\hline\\
\omega&= \text{Angular Frequency}\\
\kappa&= \text{Torsion Constant}\\
I&= \text{Moment of Inertia}
\end{aligned}
$
## Frequency
We can then solve for frequency:
$
\begin{aligned}
f&= \frac1{2\pi}\sqrt\frac\kappa I\\
\hline\\
\omega&= \text{Angular Frequency}\\
\kappa&= \text{Torsion Constant}\\
I&= \text{Moment of Inertia}
\end{aligned}
$
## Angular Displacement as a Function of Time
We can also give the angular displacement as a function of time:
$
\begin{aligned}
\theta(t)&= \Theta\cos(\omega t+\phi)\\
\hline\\
\theta&= \text{Angular Displacement}\\
\Theta&= \text{Angular Amplitude}\\
\omega&= \text{Angular Frequency}\\
\phi&= \text{Phase Shift}
\end{aligned}
$
