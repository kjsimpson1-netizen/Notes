The speed of a sound wave depends upon a constant of the medium, as well as the density of the medium. We can find the speed of [[Sound Waves]] in fluids using the following equations:
## Fluids
$$\begin{align*}
&\textcolor{red}{\text{Fluids}}\\
v&= \sqrt\frac B\rho\\
\hline\\
v&= \text{Velocity}\\
B&= \text{Bulk Modulus}\\
\rho&= \text{Density}
\end{align*}$$
## Solids
We can also find the speed of sound waves though solids using the Young's modulus of the material, as well as the density
$$\begin{align*}
&\textcolor{red}{\text{Solids}}\\
v&= \sqrt\frac E\rho\\
\hline\\
v&= \text{Velocity}\\
E&= \text{Young's Modulus}\\
\rho&= \text{Density}
\end{align*}$$
## Gasses
For gasses, we need to consider the temperature of the gas, the molar mass, as well as the ratio of heat capacities:
$$\begin{align*}
&\textcolor{red}{\text{Gasses}}\\
v&= \sqrt\frac {\gamma RT}\rho\\
\hline\\
v&= \text{Velocity}\\
\gamma&= \text{Ratio of Heat Capacities}\\
R&= \text{Gas Constant}\\
T&= \text{Absolute Temperature}\\
M&= \text{Molar Mass}
\end{align*}$$
