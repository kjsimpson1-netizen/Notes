This is when there is energy added to an object undergoing [[Simple Harmonic Motion]] to keep the [[Oscillations]] going, or to increase their [[Amplitude]]. The force that does this is called a **driving force**.
If we apply this driving force with any angular [[Frequency]], this is called a **forced oscillation**. It doesn't necessarily matter what the [[Frequency]] is, because the driving force can change the [[Angular Frequency]] of the oscillations. We can find the [[Amplitude]] due to a driving force with the following equation:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
A&= \frac{F_\text{max}}{\sqrt{(k-m{\omega_d}^2)^2+b^2{\\
\omega_d}^2}}\\
\hline\\
A&= \text{Amplitude}\\
F_\text{max}&= \text{Force}\\
k&= \text{Force Constant}\\
m&= \text{Mass}\\
\omega_d&= \text{Driving Angular Frequency}\\
b&= \text{Damping Constant}
\end{aligned}
$
$
The [[Amplitude]] will be the largest when $\omega_d=\sqrt\frac km$, and the height at this point is proportional to $\frac1b$, therefore the less damping, the higher the peak.
