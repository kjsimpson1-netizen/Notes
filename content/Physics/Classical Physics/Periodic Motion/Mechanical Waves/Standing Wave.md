These are sinusoidal waves that travel along a string that is fixed on one end, with an object making it undergo [[Simple Harmonic Motion]] on the other. These waves end up having **[[Physics/Classical Physics/Periodic Motion/Periodic Motion Definitions/Nodes]]** that never move and **[[Antinodes]]** that will have the maximal [[Amplitude]]. Because the wave doesn't appear to be moving longitudinally, and rather just transversely along the string, these are called standing waves.
In reality, there is a reflected wave that is undergoing [[Interference]] with the non reflected waves, and the final wave is the [[Superposition of Waves]]. 
We can express these waves as a function of time and position:
>[!Note]
>The amplitude of a standing wave is twice that of the original travelling waves
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
y(x,t)&= (A_{SW}\sin(kx)) \cdot\sin(\omega t)\\
\hline\\
A&= \text{Amplitude}\\
k&= \text{Wave Number}\\
x&= \text{Position}\\
\omega&= \text{Angular Frequency}\\
t&= \text{Time}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$

The [[Physics/Classical Physics/Periodic Motion/Periodic Motion Definitions/Nodes]] that you see on the string will occur following the expressions:
>[!Note]
>These are are mathematically equivalent, as $k= \frac{2\pi}\lambda$
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
x&= \begin{cases}
0,\frac\pi k,\frac{\pi}k,\frac{3\pi}k...\\
0, \frac\lambda 2, \frac{2\lambda }2, \frac{3\lambda}2...
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
