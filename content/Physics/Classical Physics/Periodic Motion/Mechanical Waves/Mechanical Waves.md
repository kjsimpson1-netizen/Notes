A mechanical wave is any wave that travels through some material. The material is called the **medium**. As the medium has a wave pass through it, they change in various ways depending on the type of wave. Some example of types of waves are wiggling strings, sound waves or waves of water. The 2 main types of waves are **transverse and longitudinal**.
## Transverse Waves
These are waves that have the displacement of the medium being perpendicular to the waves motion, such as a wiggling rope.
## Longitudinal Waves
These are waves where the displacement of the medium is parallel to the direction of the wave, such as sound waves. 
>[!Note]
>Waves in water are both transverse and longitudinal
## Periodic Longitudinal Waves
An example of this is when there is a compression of any fluid. Assuming we have a piston that moves, when it initially moves, there will be a greater density of the fluid near the piston. This region of higher density, called a compression, then propagates outwards. However, this compression also leaves a region of lower density behind, and this area is called **rarefaction**.
