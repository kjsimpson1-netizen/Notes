This is when a material changes state (solid, gas, liquid). For any given [[Pressure]], a phase change takes place at a definite temperature. For example, when we add [[Heat]] to ice, the temperature of the ice does not increase, rather the energy is converted into the phase change from ice to water (still at 0$\degree$C). 
The amount of energy required to fully change the state of 1kg of an object is called the latent heat. The equation for the amount of heat required to be added (or removed to return it from a liquid to a solid) to the object to change it's state is given by the expression:
$
\begin{aligned}
Q&= \pm mL
\end{aligned}
$
Where $m$ is the constant of latent fusion, and the $\pm$ symbol indicates that the process is reversible for going the other way down the states. Also, the freezing temperature is the same as the melting temperature for all materials. The phase change is not complete until all the matter has changed states, then it can continue raising the temperature. 
Mammals use the latent heat of evaporation to stay cool, by having a thing layer of water that takes a massive amount of heat to evaporate will cool the mammal overall. 
## Sublimation
Whenever a substance skips the solid state and changes directly from solid to gas, this is called sublimation, and this has a different constant, the heat of sublimation.
