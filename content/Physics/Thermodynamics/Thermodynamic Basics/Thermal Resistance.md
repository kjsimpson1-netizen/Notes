This is for thermal insulation in buildings, denoted by the symbol $R$. Every material has it's own specific R-value, and the [[Heat]] flow can be given by:
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
H=\frac{A(T_H-T_C)}R
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
Using the equation in [[Conduction]], we can derive the following equation for the R-value:
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
R&= \frac Lk
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
With L being the thickness of the slab, and the k being the thermal conductivity constant. The higher that the R-value of the material is, the worse that heat is conducted through it. 
>[!Note]
>The units of these are strange, being $\frac{ft^2\cdot\degree F\cdot h}{BTU}$.
