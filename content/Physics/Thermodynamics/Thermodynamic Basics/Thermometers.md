A thermometer actually only measures it's own temperature, but due to the [[Laws of Thermodynamics]], it will show the temperature of the environment directly around it as well once it comes into [[Thermal Equilibrium]]. 
### Mercury Thermometer
This thermometer works by having a narrow capillary in a glass tube with a reservoir of mercury at the bottom, and when the mercury is heated, it expands and goes farther up the capillary. However there can be a small discrepancy due to the fact that the [[Linear Expansion]] of mercury based on temperature is not quite linear, therefore the higher the temperature is the greater the discrepancy. (Because the amount that is expands changes for every degree, the temperatures will be underestimated by the thermometer)
### Bimetallic Strip Thermometers
When two strips of metal with two different coefficients of [[Linear Expansion]] are attached together and they are heated, they will concave in the direction of the metal with a smaller expansion coefficient as it will expand less. This can also be used as a thermometer by attaching it to a dial. 
### Resistance Thermometer
These thermometers use the principle that the conductivity of a metal changes based upon the temperature that the metal is at, therefore it can be used as a method to measure temperature.
### Infrared Thermometer
This thermometer detects the amount of infrared light that is emitted from an object, and because the amount of infrared light emitted is a property of the temperature of the object due to blackbody Radiation, it can be used to determine the temperature of the object. These are often used as medical thermometers as they are easy to use and accurate. These also do not need to be in [[Thermal Equilibrium]] with the object. 
### Constant-Volume Gas Thermometer
The problem with the previous thermometers is that while they perfectly agree at 0$\degree$C and 100$\degree$C, they may not perfectly agree at every intermediate value. This is where the constant-volume gas thermometer comes in, which is almost ideal. 
This thermometer works by using the idea that as an ideal gases temperature increases, the [[Pressure]] (the kinetic energy of will increase as well), and then use these measurements to infer the temperatures. These are impractical for every day use though, as they are large and take long periods of time to come to [[Thermal Equilibrium]], therefore they are rather used to calibrate other thermometers.
