This is the [[Stress]] that is caused when an object is clamped at a fixed length, then subjected to a change of temperature. The object wanting to expand is prevented from doing so by the clamps, and this [[Strain]] can be enough to break the rod. This is why there are gaps in rail road rods and bridges.
To calculate the thermal stress in a rod, we compute the amount the rod would expand (or contract) if allowed to using the [[Linear Expansion]], then find the [[Stress]] needed to stretch it back to it's original length.
$$
$$\begin{aligned}
\left(\frac{\Delta L}{L_0}\right)_\text{thermal}&= \alpha\Delta T
\end{aligned}$$
$$
The tension must increase (or decrease) by an amount that is just enough to produce an equal and opposite force in the opposite direction following [[Newton's Third Law]]. We can derive an equation using [[Young's Modulus]]
$$
$$\begin{aligned}
Y&= \frac{\frac{F}{A}}{\frac{\Delta L}{L_0}}\\
&\text{so,}\\
\left(\frac{\Delta L}{L_0}\right)_\text{tension}&= \frac{F}{AY}
\end{aligned}$$
$$
If the length is to be constant, the total fractional change in length must be zero. From these equations, this means that:
$$
$$\begin{aligned}
\left(\frac{\Delta L}{L_0}\right)_\text{thermal}&+\left(\frac{\Delta L}{L_0}\right)_\text{tension}=\alpha\Delta T+\frac{F}{AY}=0&
\end{aligned}$$
$$
We can then solve this equation for tensile [[Stress]] ($\frac FA$) to keep the rod's length constant, giving us this final equation for thermal stress:
$$
$$\begin{aligned}
\frac FA=-Y\alpha\Delta T
\end{aligned}$$
$$
The negative in the equation indicates that it is a normal force, acting against the [[Force]] attempting to squeeze or pull the object.
