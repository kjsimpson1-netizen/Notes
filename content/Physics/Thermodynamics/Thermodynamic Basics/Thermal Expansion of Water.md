Water in the temperature range of 0-4$\degree$C will actually decrease in volume rather than increasing, meaning that it's coefficient of [[Volumetric Expansion]] will actually be negative. Above 4$\degree$C, water will expand, therefore water will be the densest at 4$\degree$C. This means that the water at the bottom of lakes is always 4$\degree$C, because that's where the densest water will go. Water also expands when it is frozen, compared to most materials which will contract when they are frozen because the water will form a lattice structure which will take up more space. This means that when lakes freeze, the ice remains at the surface because it is the least dense and the denser water remains at the bottom. If this was any other substance, the solid would sink to the bottom and the lake would freeze from the bottom up.
