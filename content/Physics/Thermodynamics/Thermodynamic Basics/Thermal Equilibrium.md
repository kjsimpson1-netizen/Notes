A system is in thermal equilibrium if all the objects in the system are at the exact same temperature and there is no [[Heat]] transfer between them. This does not necessarily mean that all the objects have the same quantity of energy, but just that they all have the same temperature. 
### Brick Example
The atoms in all solid objects will oscillate, but not move around like gasses. As the temperature of the brick increases, the overall vibration of the molecules will increase. This can be caused by the warmer air (oscillating more) will give some of it's energy to the brick, therefore increasing the temperature of the brick overall, while in turn decreasing the overall energy of the warmer air. After enough time has passed and if we assume a closed system, eventually the brick and it's surroundings will all be the same temperature, with the brick warming up and the air cooling down.
