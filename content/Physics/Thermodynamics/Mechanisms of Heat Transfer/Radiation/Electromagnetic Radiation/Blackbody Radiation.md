# Emission and Absorption Spectrums
![[BlackbodyRadiationSpectrum.png]]
These lines occur when the electrons of the atoms of gas are excited by another electron hits them. The electron that hits them elevates the orbiting electron to a random orbital shell. The electron, now in a higher orbital, will "fall" back down through the orbits. The "fall" between every orbit, corresponds with a different colour, and the electron can have a different amount of steps to fall down to it's resting orbital. There is a randomness of the order which this happens, and this is due to the uncertainty of quantum mechanics. Therefore, we do not know the exact colour that will be emitted each time, and rather there area a multitude of different colours. This is what is represented by the spectra. 
Each element also has it's own specific colours that it can emit, and certain colours cannot be obtained from certain colours. 
Hitting an electron with the specific wavelength of light also causes it to elevate to a higher level, and then this is re-emitted in other random directions, causing black lines on the spectrum. This is the absorption spectrum.
