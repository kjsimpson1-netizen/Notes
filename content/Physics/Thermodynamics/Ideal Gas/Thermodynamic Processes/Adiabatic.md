These are [[Pressure Volume Processes]] in which there is no [[Heat]] flow into or out of the system, therefore $Q=0$. There doesn't necessarily have to be 0 heat flow, just required to be enough insulation that there isn't adequate heat flow to effect the system. This can either happen due to high insulation, or that the process happens so quickly that there isn't enough time for [[Heat]] flow.
We can represent this using the equation from the [[1st Law of Thermodynamics]]:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
\Delta U&= Q-W&\textcolor{red}{\text{Q=0}}&\\
\Delta U&= -W
\end{aligned}
$
$
This means that for any change in internal energy, there will be a negative change in work. 
>[!Note]
>The temperature of the system can still change, but there is no flow of heat.

This is the [[Pressure Volume Processes]] for an adiabatic system. The overall shape of the curve is called an adiabat. 
![[Adiabatic.png]]
During adiabatic compression, the gas is compressed, therefore doing negative work. Following the equation above, this gives us that there is a positive increase in [[Internal Energy]], and therefore a rise in temperature. This is the opposite in expansion. 
## Work done by Adiabatic Processes
The equation for [[Work]] done by an adiabatic process can be given by the equations:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
W&= \begin{cases}
nC_V(T_1-T_2)\\
\frac{C_V}{R}(p_1V_1-p_2V_2)\\
\frac1{\gamma-1}(p_1V_1-p_2V_2)
\end{cases}
\end{aligned}
$
$
>[!Note]
>Because we assumed that this is an adiabatic process and that there is little to no heat, this condition must be met for the approximations to be accurate.

## Relationship between Pressure and Volume
$
$
\begin{aligned}
P_1{V_1}^\gamma&= P_2{V_2}^\gamma\\
\\
P&:\text{ Pressure}\\
V&:\text{ Volume}\\
\gamma&:\text{ Ratio of Specific Heats}
\end{aligned}
$
$
>[!Note]
>For a monatomic gas, the $\gamma$ value is $\frac53$

$
$
\begin{aligned}
T_1V_1^{\gamma-1}&= T_2V_2^{\gamma-1}
\end{aligned}
$
$
