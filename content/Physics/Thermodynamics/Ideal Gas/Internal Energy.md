This is the principle that all matter contains molecules with certain velocities (kinetic energy) and [[Potential Energy]]. The sum of these two, of all the molecules in the system, can be though of as the internal energy, represented by a $U$. We can find the change in $U$, or $\Delta U$. We can find this by taking the difference of the initial internal energy. 
We can also represent the internal energy of a system by the summation of the [[Work]] done <u>by</u> the system and the amount of heat in the system (as seen in the [[1st Law of Thermodynamics]]).
>[!Note]
>A shortcoming of this equation is that we can find the change in the internal energy, however we don't know either the initial or the final, we can't find the other
## Finding Internal Energy
Since summing up all the individual energies of every particle in an object would take way too long, we can solve for internal energy another way, using the [[1st Law of Thermodynamics]]. What we have found through experiment, is that while [[Heat]] and [[Work]] are both dependent of their paths, the $\Delta U$ is independent of it's path, meaning that no matter how the system gets from state one to state two, the change in internal energy is the same. 
>[!Note]
>The internal energy of a system is determined solely by the temperature and how many moles are present, not by the pressure or the volume that it occupies

## Equation of $\Delta U$
$$
$$\begin{aligned}
\Delta U&= nC_vT\\
U&= \frac32nRT
\end{aligned}$$
$$
