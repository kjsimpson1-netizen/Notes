We can think of a refrigerator as a [[Heat Engine]] operating in reverse, because where the heat engine takes heat from a hot reservoir and brings it to a cool one, a refrigerator does the opposite.
>[!Note]
The sign convention of refrigerators is that the cold reservoir will have a positive sign, while the work and the hot reservoir will have a negative sign. Like heat engines, since it is a cyclic process, [[Internal Energy]] is 0, so we can express the [[1st Law of Thermodynamics]] as:
$$
\begin{aligned}
|Q_H|&= |Q_C|+|W|
\end{aligned}
$$
## Coefficient of performance
Like the coefficient of efficiency in [[Heat Engine]], this shows how effectively that the refrigerator is at transferring energy. We can express it as:
$$
\begin{aligned}
K&= \frac{|Q_C|}{|Q_H|-|Q_C|}
\end{aligned}
$$
We can also express this as the amount of heat that we remove with respect to the power we put in:
$$
\begin{aligned}
K&= \frac HP
\end{aligned}
$$
The process that the refrigerator goes through is:
1. [[Adiabatic]] compression
2. Fluid releases some [[Heat]] to the environment
3. Fluid expands adiabatically
4. Fluid absorbs heat from refrigerator
5. Repeats cycle
## Heat Pump
This is the same as a refrigerator, but inside out, moving the heat the other way
