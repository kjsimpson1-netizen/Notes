This is an example of an ideal [[Heat Engine]], and it is the example of the highest efficiency possible using a hot reservoir and a cold reservoir. For this to have the maximal efficiency, we must avoid all [[Irreversible Process]]. 
Therefore, we must avoid heat flow through a finite temperature drop. This means that when the [[Heat Engine]] takes in energy from the hot source, the engine must also be at the hot temperature. This is the same during the contact with the cold reservoir. This means that both of these processes must be [[Isothermal]], otherwise these would be both [[Irreversible Process]].
Also, during the transition between the two of the isothermal processes, there must be no conversion between the [[Ideal Gas]] and the hot and cold reservoirs, as this would be another [[Irreversible Process]]. This means that both of these processes must be [[Adiabatic]].
Since all of this is true, there is no other conventional engine more efficient than a Carnot engine. The key to this is that the process is completely reversible, and is just a refrigerator when ran in reverse. 
>[!Note]
>All Carnot engines that operate between the same two temperatures will also have the same efficiency, regardless of the nature of the working substance. 
## Efficiency
The efficiency of the Carnot cycle can be derived through many equations for the laws of thermodynamics. At the end, we will end up with the efficiency being equal to the ratio of the hot reservoir in to the cold reservoir. 
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
e_\text{Carnot}&=1-\frac{T_C}{T_H}
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
Therefore the efficiency of a [[Heat Engine]] can never be 100%, unless the temperature of the cold reserve is 0 (which is impossible).
## Coefficient of Performance
We can also express the coefficient of a refrigerator, $K_\text{Carnot}$, with a similar expression:
$$
$$
\begin{aligned}
K_\text{Carnot}&= \frac{T_C}{T_H}-T_C
\end{aligned}
$$
$$
This means that when the difference between the two temperatures is small, a lot of [[Heat]] can be pumped for little [[Work]]. However, the larger the difference in temperature, the more [[Work]] required to move the heat. 
>[!Note]
>The Carnot cycle must still obey the [[2nd Law of Thermodynamics]] and it cannot be attached to a Carnot refrigerator to have perpetual motion

The other key takeaway for this is that even for a regular [[Heat Engine]], the maximum efficiency is completely dependant upon the maximum and minimum temperatures.
