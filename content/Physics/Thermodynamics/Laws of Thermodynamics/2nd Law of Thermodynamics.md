This states that during the conversion of kinetic energy to heat energy is from a state of low entropy to high [[Physics/Thermodynamics/Entropy/Entropy]], therefore making it unfavourable to go the other way. 
>[!Kelvin-Planck Statement]
>It is impossible for any system to undergo a process in which it absorbs heat from a reservoir at a single temperature and converts the heat completely into mechanical work, with the system ending in the same state in which it began.

The second law is primarily to state that there is a limitation to what portion energy from heat can be converted into useful work. This states that no [[Heat Engine]] can be 100% efficient. 
This also applies more generally: a mixed drink tends to not unmix itself, air molecules tend to not collect in a single corner of a room. In general, **[[Physics/Thermodynamics/Entropy/Entropy]] tends to a maximum for all reactions**.
