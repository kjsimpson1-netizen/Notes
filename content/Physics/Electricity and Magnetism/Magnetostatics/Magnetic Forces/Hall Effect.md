This is the generation of [[Voltage]] across an electrical [[Conductor]] when it carries [[Current]]. 
This happens when the current is perpendicular to the [[Magnetic Field]], and the force on the charge carriers will cause them to move to one side of the [[Conductor]], causing an [[Electric Field]] which in turn creates a [[Voltage]]. The equation for the voltage generated by the hall effect is:
$
\begin{aligned}
V&= \frac{IB}{nqd}=wvB\\
\hline\\
V&= \text{Voltage}\\
B&= \text{Magnetic Field}\\
n&= \text{Charge Carrier Density}\\
q&= \text{Charge}\\
d&= \text{Thickness of Conductor}\\
w&= \text{Width}\\
v&= \text{Drift Velocity}
\end{aligned}
$
We can also represent the term $nq$ by the following:
$
\begin{aligned}
nq&= \frac{J_xB_y}{E_z}\\
\hline\\
n&= \text{Charge Carrier Density}\\
q&= \text{Charge}\\
J&= \text{Current Density}\\
B&= \text{Magnetic Field}\\
E&= \text{Electric Field}
\end{aligned}
$
This will reach steady state once the [[Magnetic Field]] is equal to the [[Electric Field]].
