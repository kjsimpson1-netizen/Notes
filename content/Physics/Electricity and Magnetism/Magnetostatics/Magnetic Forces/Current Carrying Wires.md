## One Wire
The [[Current]] through a wire is the movement of [[Electric Charge]], this means that there is a force upon the wire as well. The force felt by the wire is given by:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
\vec{\mathbf{F}}= I\vec l\times\vec{\mathbf{B}}&\qquad d\vec{\mathbf{F}}= I\,d\vec l\times\vec{\mathbf{B}}\\
\hline\\
F&= \text{Force}\\
I&= \text{Current}\\
\vec l&= \text{Length}\\
\vec{\mathbf{B}}&= \text{Magnetic Field}
\end{aligned}
$
$
>[!Note]
>We can also integrate the second expression to find the force upon a wire with changing values

### Infinite Wire
For a single wire, that is infinitely long, this gives us:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
\vec B(r)&= -\frac{\mu_0I}{2\pi r}\hat k\\
\hline\\
\vec B&= \text{Magnetic Field}\\
\mu_0&= \text{Magnetic Constant}\\
I&= \text{Current}\\
r&= \text{Distance from Wire}
\end{aligned}
$
$
### Semi-Infinite Wire
For a respectably long, however finite wire:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
\vec B(r)&= -\frac{\mu_0I}{4\pi r}\hat k\\
\hline\\
\vec B&= \text{Magnetic Field}\\
\mu_0&= \text{Magnetic Constant}\\
I&= \text{Current}\\
r&= \text{Distance from Wire}
\end{aligned}
$
$
## Two Wires
Two current carrying wires will exert a force upon each other through the [[Magnetic Field]]. We can then express this with:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
\frac FL&= \frac{\mu_0I_1I_2}{2\pi d}\\
\hline\\
F&= \text{Force}\\
L&= \text{Length}\\
\mu_0&= \text{Magnetic Constant}\\
I&= \text{Current}\\
d&= \text{Distance}
\end{aligned}
$
$
We can determine the direction of the force from the right hand rule. This force will either be attractive or repulsive between the two wires depending upon the [[Current]] direction. 
>[!Note]
>As a general rule, currents flowing in the same direction will repel and opposite directions will attract
