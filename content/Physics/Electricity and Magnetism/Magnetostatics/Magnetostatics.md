The magnetism that we observe is a property that comes from a quantum mechanic quality of electrons called *spin*. A magnet magnetizes another material by aligning the spin of it's electrons with it's own. 
Magnets have north ends and south ends, and the magnetic fields come from the north end of the magnet and go around to the south end. The symbol for magnetism is "B" and their units are Tesla (T).
## Types of Magnetism
### Diamagnetism
When these materials are placed inside exterior [[Magnetic Field]], they oppose the direction of the exterior [[Magnetic Field]]. These are repelled by magnets, and they are temporary. 
### Paramagnetic
When these materials are placed inside exterior magnetic fields, they are attracted to the magnet as their [[Magnetic Field]] aligns. These are temporary, and it becomes weaker the higher the temperature, as the thermal energy tends to randomize the electrons spins.
### Ferromagnetic
These objects are attracted to other exterior magnetic fields, and when they have a [[Magnetic Field]] run through them, they develop their own permanent magnetic field. The three ferrimagnetic materials are cobalt, iron and nickel. Inside of these materials, there are magnetic domains where all of the electrons have the same spin direction. However, without a [[Magnetic Field]], these domains are randomly oriented and cancel out to be 0.
