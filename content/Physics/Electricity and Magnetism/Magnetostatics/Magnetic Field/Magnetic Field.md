This is the field, also known as [[Magnetic Flux]] density, that magnets or moving [[Electric Charge]] exert on the space around them, very similar to how [[Electric Charge]] will induce an [[Electric Field]] in the space around it. This magnetic field will then exert a force on any other moving charges or [[Current]] that is in the field. 
>[!Note]
>Magnetic field is a vector field, as every point on the field can have the same magnitude but different directions

The magnetic field is represented by $\vec{\bf{B}}$ and the units of magnetic field are the Tesla (T), or the Gauss (G), which is $\times10^{4}$ smaller.
>[!Note]
The definition of positive magnetic field is to point from the north pole into it's south pole. 
## Magnetic Field Lines
These are lines that are drawn to show the direction of the electric field in that region. If the field goes into the page there, it is represented with an "$\times$", and if it comes out then it is represented by a "$\bullet$". The density of the field lines on a page also tell us about the magnitude of the field, the denser the lines the stronger the field. These field lines will never intersect with one another, and they <u>must</u> always form closed loops.
