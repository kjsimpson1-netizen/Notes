## In Series
This is the equivalent to the [[Resistance]] of an [[Alternating Current]] circuit. The current in an [[Alternating Current]] circuit will always take the path of lowest impedance, which can be expressed by:
$$\begin{align*}
Z&= \sqrt{R^2+(X_L-X_C)^2}\\
\hline\\
Z&= \text{Impedance}\\
R&= \text{Resistance}\\
X_L&= \text{Inductive Reactance}\\
X_C&= \text{Capacitive Reactance}
\end{align*}$$
## In parallel
If the components are all in parallel, then the overall impedance can be given by:
$$\begin{align*}
Z&= \sqrt{\left(\frac1R\right)^2+\left(\frac1{X_L}-\frac1{X_C}\right)^2}\\
\hline\\
Z&= \text{Impedance}\\
R&= \text{Resistance}\\
X_L&= \text{Inductive Reactance}\\
X_C&= \text{Capacitive Reactance}
\end{align*}$$
## Other Setups
If they are in a setup other than strictly in series or parallel, then each of the impedances of each branch can be found then they can be added together with the following:
### Series
$$\begin{align*}
Z_T&= \sum_{i=1} Z_i
\end{align*}$$
### Parallel
$$\begin{align*}
\frac1{Z_T}&= \sum_{i=1}\frac1{Z_{i}}
\end{align*}$$
