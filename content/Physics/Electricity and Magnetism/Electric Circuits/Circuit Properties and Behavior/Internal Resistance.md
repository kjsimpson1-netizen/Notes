All real cells of batteries contain a small amount of [[Resistance]]. The terminal voltage is the amount of energy that gets out of the battery, and it can be found by subtracting the [[Voltage]] lost to the internal resistance from the [[Electromotive Force]]. 
$$\begin{align*}
V_{term}&= \mathcal{E}\pm Ir_I\\
\hline\\
V_{term}&= \text{Terminal Voltage}\\
\mathcal{E}&= \text{Electromotive Force}\\
r_1&= \text{Resistance}
\end{align*}$$
There is also [[Electrical Power]] lost to the [[Internal Resistance]], and this is why batteries will heat up as they're being used.
>[!Note]
>The positive in this expression is only used when the battery is being recharged.
