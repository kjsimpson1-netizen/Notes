Current is the flow of electrons through a circuit, measured in coulombs per second. The units for these are amps (A). Ammeters are wired as series, not in parallel, as the ammeter has no [[Resistance]].
>[!Note]
When a battery "dies", it doesn't run out of electrons, it actually just runs out of chemical reactants. The capacity of the battery is measured in amp-hours. 
## Why it happens
When there is no electric field, the electrons in a conductor will randomly move around at speeds of up to $10^6$ m/s, however since their motion is completely random, there is no net motion. However, if we introduce a current, then the electrons begin to move in a given direction, however, not at the speed of the free electron speed, as the electrons will collide with the stationary ions and be slowed down to a speed of $10^{-4}$ m/s. However, when the switch is flicked, the electrons all begin moving in unison at the speed of light propagating from the source. 
## Direction
The electric field will do work on the moving charges, and this work translates into both moving the electrons, as well as heating up the conductor through the collisions with the stationary molecules. 
>[!Note]
>While metals only have their electrons moving as the atoms are fixed, solutions and plasmas can have both positive and negative charges moving

The direction of the movement of the electrons is the same as the direction of the electric field force. However, current is declared as the movement of positive charges, the opposite way that electrons flow.
