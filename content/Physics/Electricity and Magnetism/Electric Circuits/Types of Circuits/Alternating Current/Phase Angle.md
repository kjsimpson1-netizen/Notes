## In Series
The [[Current]] and the [[Voltage]] in [[Alternating Current]] circuits will be out of phase, if the phase angle is positive, then the voltage leads the current, and vice versa for negative. The phase angle between the two can be expressed as:
$
\begin{aligned}
\tan\phi&= \frac{\omega L-\frac1{\omega C}}R=\frac{X_L-X_C}{R}\\
\hline\\
\phi&= \text{Phase Angle}\\
\omega&= \text{Angular Frequnecy}\\
C&= \text{Capitance}\\
R&= \text{Resistance}\\
X_L&= \text{Inductive Reactance}\\
X_C&= \text{Inductive Capacitance}
\end{aligned}
$
This can also be found using a [[Phasor Diagram]]
>[!Note]
>If the resistor or the inductor are missing from the circuit, we set their corresponding values to 0; however, if the capacitor is missing, we set the value to $\infty$ due to the lack of potential difference

## In Parallel
This is the same as in series, except with all the values reciprocated:
$
\begin{aligned}
\tan\phi&= \frac{\frac1{X_L}-\frac1{X_C}}R\\
\hline\\
\phi&= \text{Phase Angle}\\
C&= \text{Capitance}\\
R&= \text{Resistance}\\
X_L&= \text{Inductive Reactance}\\
X_C&= \text{Inductive Capacitance}
\end{aligned}
$
