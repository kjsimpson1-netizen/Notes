The amount of energy stored in an [[Inductor]] is:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
U&= L\int_0^Ii\,di=\frac12Li^2\\
\hline\\
U&= \text{Energy}\\
L&= \text{Inductance}\\
i&= \text{Current}
\end{aligned}
$
$
This is the integral from the initial [[Current]] value to the final current value, and once it has reached the final state, the current is no longer changing and therefore $\frac{di}{dt}=0$.
>[!Note]
>The energy stored in an inductor is stored in the magnetic field
## Magnetic Energy Density
The density of the magnetic energy stored in an [[Inductor]] can be represented by the following:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
u&= \begin{cases}
\frac{B^2}{2\mu_0}\\
\frac{B^2}{2\mu}
\end{cases}\\
\hline\\
u&= \text{Magentic Field Energy Density}\\
B&= \text{Magnetic Field}\\
\mu_0&= \text{Magnetic Constant}\\
\mu&= \text{Permeability}
\end{aligned}
$
$
If the material inside the inductor is not a vacuum, the $\mu_0$ gets replaced by $\mu$, being the permeability of the material.
