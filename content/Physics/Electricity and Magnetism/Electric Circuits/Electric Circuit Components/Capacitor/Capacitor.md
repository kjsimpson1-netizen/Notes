Capacitors will store electrical energy in the form of [[Electric Field]] between the two plates of the capacitor. The measurement of how good a capacitor is the [[Capacitance]], and the units are Farads (F).
## Electrical Charge
The [[Electric Charge]] of the [[Capacitor]] can never truly reach the [[Voltage]] of the battery, as as the [[Electric Charge]] on the plates increases, so does the push against more charge being stored . The charge on the plate of the [[Capacitor]] is shown as a [[Voltage]]. The larger the capacitor the more coulombs of energy will be stored for the same input energy.
$$\begin{align*}
q(t)&= C\cdot V(t)\\
\hline \\
q&= \text{Charge}\\
C&= \text{Capacitance}\\
V&= \text{Voltage}
\end{align*}$$
---
## Ratios of Voltages when Charged
#### Parallel
When there are two capacitors in parallel, if you wait forever, both of the two capacitors will have the same [[Voltage]] as the battery. And although they have the same [[Voltage]], they will have different amounts of coulombs present, and the larger capacitor will have a larger amount of coulombs.
#### Series
When there are two capacitors in series, if you wait forever, both capacitors will have the same Q value, but both will still have different C values. This means that the summation of the voltages of the two capacitors will be equal to the voltage of the battery.
