This is a more qualitative version of [[Faraday's Law]]. All this says is that the negative sign in [[Faraday's Law]] tells us that the [[Voltage]] through the loop or coil is resisting the change of [[Magnetic Flux]] through it by inducing it's own [[Magnetic Flux]], which is opposite in direction to counteract the changing flux. However, the magnitude of the induced [[Magnetic Flux]] depends upon the resistance of the coil, as the [[Current]] is a consequence of the induced electromotive force. 
$
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{E}&= -\frac{d\Phi_B}{dt}\\
\hline\\
\mathcal{E}&= \text{EMF}\\
\Phi_B&= \text{Magetic Flux}\\
t&= \text{Time}
\end{aligned}
$
>[!Note]
>If the coil has 0 resistance, being a superconductor, then the induced flux is always equal to the original flux. Therefore, there is <u>never</u> a change in the [[Magnetic Flux]] through a superconductor.
