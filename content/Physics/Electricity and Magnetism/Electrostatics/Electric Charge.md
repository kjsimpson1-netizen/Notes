An object can either have a positive or a negative charge, and the SI units of electric charge are **Coulombs**. If two objects with the same net charge are brought close to each other, they will repel and if they have the opposite charge, they will attract. These charges are caused by the protons and electrons of the atoms that make up the molecules of an object. 
>[!Note]
>The elementary charge is defined to be equal to $1.602\times10^{-19}$ Coulombs, or one coulomb is equivalent to $6\times10^{8}$ electrons 
## Conservation of Charge
This electric charge is always conserved, meaning that the algebraic sum of all the charges in a closed system will always stay the same. Therefore electrical charge is never created nor destroyed, rather just transferred between objects. 
## Magnitude of Charge
The base unit for the magnitude of a charge is that of the electron or the proton (both have the exact same charge within experimental error). This charge is always an integer multiple of the charges of the electrons and the protons that make it up, meaning that there cannot be a smaller subdivision of charge than the electron or proton.
