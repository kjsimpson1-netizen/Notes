Since the force of the two [[Electric Charge]] is equal by opposite, the net force is therefore 0. However, the net torque is nonzero as the two forces don't act upon the same line. The equation for the magnitude of torque of an [[Electric Dipole]] is a quantity related to the [[Electric Field]] and the [[Dipole Moment]]:
$$
$$\begin{aligned}
\tau&= pE\sin\phi\\
\hline\\
\tau&= \text{Torque}\\
p&= \text{Dipole Moment}\\
E&= \text{Electrical Field}\\
\phi&= \text{Angle}
\end{aligned}$$
$$
However, the torque will also have a direction, given by the cross product of the dipole moment and the [[Electric Field]], or:
$$
$$\begin{aligned}
\vec \tau&= \vec p\times\vec E\\
\hline\\
\vec\tau&= \text{Torque}\\
\vec p&= \text{Dipole Moment}\\
\vec E&= \text{Electrical Field}\\
\end{aligned}$$
$$
