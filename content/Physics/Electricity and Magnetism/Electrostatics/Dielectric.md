This is another name for an Insulator. These are objects that have very low [[Conductance|Conductance]] and they do not permit much flow of electrons through them, and therefore they will have a very high [[Resistivity]] and a very high [[Resistance]]. A good example of an insulator is air, as it does not permit the flow of electricity easily, however most non-metals are good insulators.
This is the material used in between the [[Conductor]] plates in [[Capacitor]]. This allows the distance between the plates to be minimized while still being separated. This is usually made up of a insulative material, and this is because this will increase the [[Capacitance]] of the [[Capacitor]], as the dielectric will become polarized, increasing the distribution of [[Electric Charge]].
## Dielectric Breakdown
This phenomenon is called the *dielectric breakdown* which will cause [[Corona Discharge]] in air, when the dielectric becomes polarized enough that it conducts electricity. Many material tolerate higher [[Electric Field]] than air without breaking down, and the amount of [[Electric Field]] they can withstand is called the medium's dielectric strength.
## Permittivity
This is the ability of a medium to act as a dielectric, and it is represented by:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
\epsilon&= \kappa \epsilon_0\\
\hline\\
\epsilon&= \text{Permittivity}\\
\kappa &= \text{Dielectric Constant}\\
\epsilon_0&= \text{Electric Constant}\\
\end{aligned}
$
$
>[!Note]
>The dielectric constant is dependant upon the medium
## Energy Density
We can also represent the electric energy density in a dielectric by:
$
$
\begin{aligned}
u&= \frac12\kappa\epsilon_0E^2\\
\hline\\
u&= \text{Energy Density}\\
\kappa&= \text{Dielectric Constant}\\
E&= \text{Electric Field}
\end{aligned}
$
$
