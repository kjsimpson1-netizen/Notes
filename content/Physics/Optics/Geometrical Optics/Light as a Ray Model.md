Geometrical optics use the photon model of light, and not the wave model. These photons of light can either refract (change angle), reflect, get absorbed or it can be scattered. All of these are possibilities, but for geometrical optics, the two that are focused on are the refraction and the reflection. 
## Objects
In geometrical physics, an object is a source of light rays (billions of them per second). Most things cannot produce their own light, except those that are hot enough to have blackbody radiation, and must reflect light. Most object also aren't smooth, and they randomly scatter, and this is called diffuse reflection. The lens of your eye (using [[Snell's Law]]) must bring all of these rays of light back together in a single place on your retina in order to see the object.
