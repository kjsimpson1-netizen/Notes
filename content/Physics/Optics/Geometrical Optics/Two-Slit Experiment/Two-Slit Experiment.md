This is the experiment that is in support of the wave model for light, and it is conducted by having a beam of monochromatic light from a laser hitting 2 slits and the image being cast onto a screen behind the slits. The pattern is an [[Interference]] pattern that only occurs with waves. 
## Bright Spots
We can find the regions where the light will appear the brightest using the following equation:
$
\begin{aligned}
&d\sin\theta= m\lambda\\
\hline\\
d&= \text{Slit Distance}\\
\theta&= \text{Angle of the Bright Spot}\\
m&= \text{mth Bright Spot}\\
\lambda &= \text{Wavelength}
\end{aligned}
$
These will occur where there is constructive interference from the two different slits. 
## Dark Spots
We can use the same equation as for bright spots, just modified a little to show the spot where it lines up on the nth and a half bright spot:
$
\begin{aligned}
&d\sin\theta= \left(m+\frac12\right)\lambda\\
\hline\\
d&= \text{Slit Distance}\\
\theta&= \text{Angle of the Bright Spot}\\
m&= \text{mth Bright Spot}\\
\lambda &= \text{Wavelength}
\end{aligned}
$
## Small Angles
For sufficiently small angles for the bright spots, we can use the following equation to find the position of the nth bright band:
$
\begin{aligned}
y_m&= R\frac{m\lambda}d\\
\hline\\
y&= \text{Position of the band}\\
R&= \text{Distance from slits to screen}\\
d&= \text{Distance between Slits}\\
\lambda&= \text{Wavelength}
\end{aligned}
$
