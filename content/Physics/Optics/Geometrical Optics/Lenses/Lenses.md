This is when there is a double [[Snell's Law]] refraction (on both boundaries of the lens), and causes the light to either converge or diverge, depending on the lens. Lenses will cause diffuse reflection to be concentrated upon one point on a piece of paper, forming an image (this is the apparition of the object when looking through the image). 
There are multiple types of lenses (concave and convex), and they have different characteristics following the [[Lens Equation]] and the [[Magnification]]
## Distances for Images and Objects
These are positive if they are on their respective sides of the lens, and negative if they appear to be on the other side. 
## Multiple Lenses
These can be a hassle to draw, so it is much easier to use the math, with the difference between the distance of the image of the first lens to the second lens being the second object distance. 
### Example
Find the image distance of a double convex lens system, with focal length one being 20cm, focal length 2 being 10cm, the distance between them being 48 cm, and the original object distance being 60cm from the original lens.
$$
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Finding the }d_{i1}\\
\frac1f&= \frac1{d_o}+\frac1{d_i}\\
\frac1{20}&= \frac1{60}+\frac1{d_i}\\
d_{i1}&= 30\;cm\\
\\
&\text{Second }d_o\\
48-30&=18\\
\\
 &\text{Finding the }d_{i2}\\
\frac1f&= \frac1{d_o}+\frac1{d_i}\\
\frac1{10}&= \frac1{18}+\frac1{d_i}\\
d_i&= 22.5cm
\end{aligned}
$$
To find the overall [[Magnification]], the two magnifications can be multiplied together.
$$
\begin{aligned}
-\frac12\times-\frac54&= \frac58
\end{aligned}
$$
Because it is positive, the final image will appear upright and little small because it is less than 1. 
### Note
If the first image appears on the other side of the second lens, then the second lens will have to use a negative object distance in the math.
