## Organization
- Usually in presentations, the simpler content should come earlier in order to introduce the topics
- The outline of the presentation usually directly follows the opening slide, however it doesn't necessarily have to if there is a good enough reason. 
### Outline
In the outline slide of the presentation, we do not need to include things like the title slide, or the outline slide, as this is redundant. We should also keep the amount of topics listed limited and more general, with sub-points below each overarching theme. 
### Text
Try to avoid having very text heavy slides, as the [[Audience]] will not want to read it all and may zone out and not pay attention. Rather, it is much better to just have short bullets, then to read out other things. Also, use formal grammar.
>[!Note]
>Don't just read out the slides, as this may frustrate [[Audience]] members
### Backgrounds
The plainer the better, as this won't distract the viewer from the more important content, and it will improve the data-ink ratio. Avoid things in the corners, as this will draw the attention away from the centre. Also, don't use backgrounds that will have a low contrast between the text and the background. 
### Images
Only include images if they are directly connected to the slide content in order to not distract the viewers with irrelevant content. 
### Transitions/Animations
Avoid unnecessary animations and other visual effects, unless they directly improve the presentation in a way that allows the speaker to introduce different parts one at a time. 
## Slide Set-ups
### Title-Content
This is when there is a short, descriptive title at the top of the slide with the content reinforcing the slide down below. 
### Assertion-Evidence
This is when there is a full sentence to two that states a claim, and then the body of the slide reinforces it with either text or images/graphs.
