Doing a full life cycle assessment requires the complete knowledge of each individual component of the product and how it impacts each part of the life cycle, as well as precisely how much of each material is used and how it is integrated into the pencil. This means that it is a highly rigorous process involving lots of [[Life Cycle Thinking]].
>[!Note]
>LCA's are primarily used on already existing products, however there are exceptions to be discussed. This is because there must be a high required degree of knowledge on the product, impacts must be known and quantified and it mainly focuses on the environment. 

The overall main steps of an LCA are:
1. [[Goal definition and scope]]
2. [[Inventory analysis]]
3. [[Impact Assessment]]
4. [[Interpretation]] of each of the previous steps
