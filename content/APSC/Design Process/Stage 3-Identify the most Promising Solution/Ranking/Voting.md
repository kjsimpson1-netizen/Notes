This is a method of [[Ranking]] the ideas based off the chances of them working. 
>[!Note]
>This method will only work if all the team members have a good understanding of the problem, the concepts, and the [[Evaluation Criteria]].

There are multiple different methods of voting, each one with it's own pros and cons:
## Individual Voting
In this method, each team member will vote for whichever concept they believe has the highest chance of working. These should be done independently to avoid group thinking. Each person can get a predetermined amount of votes, that they can distribute how they please. From this point, each concept gets the total amount of votes that it received totalled up, and whichever have the highest can get advanced forward for more detailed analysis. 
>[!Warning]
>Just because one idea has the highest amount of votes does not necessarily mean that it is the most promising, as this is a simple qualitative test, and note rigorous and quantitative. 

## Ranked Voting
In this style of voting, each concept is ranked from $n-1$, with higher numbers corelating with better scores, and with $n$ being the quantity of ideas. The concepts with the highest scores will advance
## Pairwise Comparison
This is when each concept is compared to each other concept, 1 is awarded for being better than the other, and $\frac12$ points are awarded to each for a tie. The highest amount of points advances
