Engineering design is the process through which engineering knowledge and skills are applied to solve real world (meaningful to people), open ended problems (there isn't a single solution, or a single path to get there). Our job is to understand and define the problem, then work our way to the solution.
## Novice Approach
This is the approach that most people will take, and it is essentially trial and error, with repeated revisions of the same problem, reimplementing after every solution. While this works well for inexpensive and small projects, it's not feasible for larger, more costly ones. Even if this method works it will likely not optimal and will require more time, money and resources. 
## Systematic Process
This is more likely to be successful.
>[!Note]
>Due to [[Iteration]], there is no guarantee that this will be a linear process from 1-5, while it is true that each must be visited on the way there, there is no guarantee you won't have to return to a previous stage. 
### Stage 0
This is finding the problem
>[!Note]
>In ASPC100, this will always be provided for us
### Stage 1-Study and Clarify
In practice, engineering problems are often not well defined. For examples, clients may not know the full concept of the problem, and they may not know things like [[Stakeholders]] involved, the [[Needs]] these [[Stakeholders]] will have, and existing technologies, 
### Stage 2-Generate Possible Solutions
The key here is to identify as many ways as possible to solve the problem. The broader the range of ideas here, the more likely it will be that there will be a great solution in the end. 
>[!Warning]
>Do not just evaluate the idea immediately, rather just generate a multitude of ideas
### Stage 3-Identify the most Promising Solution
Our job here is to narrow down the numerous solutions generated in stage 2 until one is identified that we will pursue. 
### Stage 4-Develop and Test Solution
Here we will analyse, refine and develop the chosen solution from stage 3 using science, math and engineering tools
### Stage 5-Implement the Solution
This is the final construction or detailing of the solution
## Support for the Design Process
As we proceed through the stages, our costs (effort, money and resources) required to actually carry out the process exponentially increases. This states that the early stages will cost much, much less than the later stages.  
However, the costs committed to the project will more so resemble a logarithmic curve, as these are future costs and they are frozen based on the decisions that we make early on in the design process. What this shows is that most of the decisions made early in the project, will decide our spending later in the project. 
While the decisions and actions done early on in the project have a very low impact on actual costs, they will have a very large impact on the costs committed to the project. 
This is why a formal design process is better, as it takes advantage of the early stages of the design process where the costs are low.
