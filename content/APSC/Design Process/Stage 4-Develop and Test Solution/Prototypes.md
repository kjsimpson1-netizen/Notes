These are simple models or representations of a final design. One type of a prototype are sketches, and these are used because they are cheap and disposable compared to the final design, so making mistakes isn't as costly, and they can start conversations in the [[Design Process]]. 
Engineers also rely on simple mathematical models for creating prototypes, and then these models that are created based off of the mathematical models are made and tested to compare them to the mathematical models. 
Software models can also be used to visually convey concepts, as well as for producing construction drawings. 
## Dimensions of Prototypes
Generally, as we go through the design process, the prototypes we see go from being highly virtual and focused with the initial ideas (sketches), to being highly comprehensive and physical (final model). However, as we proceed across the completion dimension, we go between physical and virtual prototypes, depending on what kind of questions we need answered. 
### Completion
This axis goes from being highly focused, to being highly comprehensive.
#### Focused
These are any ideas that are highly focus on one specific element of a final design, and they are made to be highly detail intensive on that one portion
#### Comprehensive
These are prototypes that involve many different aspects of the final design, bringing them all together to get a more final picture of what the final product will be
### Reality
This axis goes from being very digital and not tangible, to be very physical
#### Virtual
These are things such as computer sketches and drawings. They are considered virtual, as they don't have any real world elements, and rather they just show concepts and ideas visually, without having anything there to actually test
#### Physical
These are prototypes that are more developed physically, and we would stereotype these as what we think of as prototypes.
