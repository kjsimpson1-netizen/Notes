The definition of sustainability is to be able to meet the needs of the present, without compromising the future. There is no single step of the [[Design Process]] that sustainability fits into, and is rather an ongoing part of it. It is best to think of this as a Venn diagram with 3 overlapping circles. The 3 dimensions of sustainability are:
## Environment
This is the maintenance of clean air, water and the biosphere as well as conservation, emissions reductions and management. 
## Society
This dimension refers to health, safety, human rights, education and opportunity. 
## Economic
This dimension refers to employments, prosperity, trade, business and innovation.
### Bearable
This is the overlap between environment and society, and is generally about green space and environmental justice. 
### Viable
This is the overlap between economy and environment, and this refers to resource efficiency and renewable energy. 
### Equitable Solutions
This is the overlap between economy and society, and it refers to job creation and local economic impact. 
### Sustainability
This is when a solution fits into all 3 of the dimensions of sustainability. 
## Four Principles of Sustainability
These are the guiding principle for sustainability, and they are:
1. Avoid removing materials at a rate faster than they naturally replenish
2. Avoid releasing substances at a rate faster than they break down
3. Avoid degrading ecosystems at a rate faster than they naturally regrow
4. Move towards happiness and well-being as a society, meeting the needs of all people
>[!Note]
>The UN also have principles of sustainability; however, theirs is more inclusive of the sustainability with respect to society.
