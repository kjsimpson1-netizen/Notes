This is the ranking of the importance of [[Stakeholders]] based upon three dimensions:
1. Power
2. Urgency
3. Legitimacy
## The 3 Dimensions
>[!Note]
>There can be differing degrees of fitting into each of the categories, for example, a group may have legal power, however if it can be over-ruled by the government, then they hold this power to a less extent.
### Power
This refers to how much [[Stakeholders]] have the ability to change the project. This power can come in the form of decision making, monetary assets, or resource contributions.
### Urgency
This refers to the magnitude of time sensitivity or [[Needs]] of a project to [[Stakeholders]]. 
### Legitimacy
This refers to the [[Stakeholders]] having a right to say in the project. These can be either legal rights or moral rights. 
## Levels of Stakeholder Salience
### Primary
These are [[Stakeholders]] who fit in all 3 of the dimensions of saliency, and are the most important to consider. To engage with these stakeholders, we can inform them of the project and monitor how they respond, provide them ample opportunities to input their opinions, as well as involving them in the decision making process. 
### Secondary 
These are [[Stakeholders]] who fit in only 2 of the 3 dimensions of saliency. While engaging with them, we can inform them of the project and monitor their response, as well as providing them opportunities to input their opinions. We can also consider giving them chances to be involved in the design process if we see fit
### Tertiary
These only fit in one of the dimensions of saliency, and are often only alerted of the project and their response is monitored; however, they may also be provided opportunity to give their opinions. 
### Non-Stakeholders
If they do not fit in any of the dimensions of saliency, then they are not [[Stakeholders]]. However, we may find that if non-stakeholders rally together, they can gain legal or social power and may become tertiary stakeholders that now must be considered. 
>[!Note]
>Even though a group of [[Stakeholders]] may fit cleanly into one of the categories above, saliency is dynamic and can change throughout a [[Design Process]]. Because of this, it is recommended to monitor [[Stakeholders]] throughout the design process to limit surprises.
